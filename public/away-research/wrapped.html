Away agent · the "they already know" experiment

Travel,
reconstructed.

One read-only Gmail connect. No forms, no questions. The agent read 2,938 travel emails going back to March 2017 and rebuilt a life of movement. Here is what it saw before anyone told it anything.

01

The home triangle

Every confirmed flight, drawn between its airports. One shape dominates without being asked: Bengaluru, Delhi, Mumbai. Thicker arc, more trips.

02

Nine years in the inbox

All travel mail per year. The line of a life getting busier - near-empty student years, a pandemic dip, then a steep climb. 2021 is missing entirely (striped): the year nobody flew.

03

Who flies you

Confirmed bookings by carrier. Air India is the clear default, more than twice the next.

04

When you fly

Flights by month of the year. One month towers over the rest - August.

05

The getaway day

Flights by day of the week. You leave on a Friday more than any other day.

06

What kind of trips

Of the cleanly-parsed records, the mix of how you move.

"They already know" is true at connect - but the raw mailbox lies.

The triangle, the airline, the nine-year arc all surface in seconds. But of 2,938 emails pulled, only about 135 were clean, structured bookings. The rest is booking-confirmation lookalikes and a flood of OTA marketing - "100% Refund on Cancellations", "Flight Delayed? Get Compensation!" - that a naive read counts as real trips.

What I'd revisit - the gap between 2,938 and 135 is the design problem. Turning an inbox into something an agent can act on needs a transactional-vs-promotional filter the first pull doesn't have. That honest gap is the point of the experiment.

Source: one read-only Gmail connect · agamagar117 · 2017–2026 · all processing local. Amounts and booking references deliberately excluded.
