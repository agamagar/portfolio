_Researched 2026-09-04. Sources numbered at the bottom; cite by number inline._

Scope: metro-city, middle-class, 2020s India, for candid lifestyle stills of someone ordering groceries on a phone. Every bullet below is either sourced (number), INFERENCE (a reasonable reading of a sourced fact), or UNVERIFIED (widely believed, no page fetched that confirms it). Where a scene came back empty the section says "nothing solid found".

## What the register actually is

The closest thing to a named aesthetic is not an interiors-journal term; it is a canon of objects. Godrej's own archive exhibition (CEPT, Ahmedabad, May to July 2025) picks three products, the steel cupboard, the chair and the refrigerator, and says they "became synonymous with the journey of middle-class India" and "symbolise modernity for countless people" [4]. Two essays list the same canon from the inside: a 2025 Zubaan piece puts the "dark green steel Godrej almirah" beside "Nikamal plastic chairs", the "Milton hot box", Zandu balm, a "glass cabinet of past glories, fridge magnet memories" and a home computer "prominently placed near the door in the drawing room" [1]; a WhatsApp forward reproduced on a blog describes the almirah in "uncertain shades of grey, brown or olive green" that make it look "like something transferred from a government records room", with "tin boxes that had once proudly carried Danish butter cookies", saris "in neat rows", winter clothes "wrapped in newspaper", "faux teak laminates" and "transparent plastic covers" [2]. Steel kitchenware is written about the same way: "sasta, sundar, tikau" (cheap, beautiful, durable), a "miracle of convenience" that arrived post-Independence and replaced brass and copper, and which "you'll hear before you see it: the metallic clang of steel utensils, of tumblers and katoris stacked into each other" [13].

So the register is: hard, wipeable, durable surfaces (steel, moulded plastic, cement-based floors, distemper walls) accumulated over decades rather than bought as a set; objects that carry brand names (Godrej, Nilkamal, Milton, Hawkins) as common nouns; and a layering of the old canon under the new (a smartphone and a quick-commerce paper bag sitting on a mosaic floor next to a Nilkamal chair). Two social-science pointers exist for anyone who wants the theory: a 2019 paper "Narrating Middle-Class Houses Across Indian History: Memory, Gender and Material Culture" (ResearchGate listing returned 403, so title only, UNVERIFIED content), and Llerena Searle's 2025 Limn essay on the upmarket end of the same register, where balconies are enclosed for floor area and the "fully contained society" copies the look of a "Four Seasons" [19]. INFERENCE: for a middle-class 2020s flat the picture sits between those two poles, mostly the first.

## Visual dictionary by scene

### Bedroom at night
- Steel almirah: original 1923 Godrej "safe cabinet" was "grey enamel with five adjustable shelves"; modern colourways include "purple, marigold, deep blue, magenta, maroon and carmine red" and a burgundy with "golden handle" [3]. Essays describe a "dark green steel Godrej almirah" with a mirror on the outside of the door [1] and "steel bodies with chrome handles" in "grey, brown or olive green" [2]. Locker, adjustable shelves, key left in the lock: INFERENCE from [2][3].
- Bed: a "double bed from the bride's side" with under-storage (box bed) [2].
- Night light: the "zero watt bulb", a low-intensity incandescent that actually draws about 12 to 15 W, used as a night lamp and around photo frames and small home temples; LED versions now exist. Source page (Bijli Bachao) resolved in search but the fetch failed on DNS, so treat the wattage figure as UNVERIFIED; the practice is widely attested.

- Textiles: Bombay Dyeing returned only retail listings, no company page fetched (UNVERIFIED as a brand claim); block-printed cotton razai quilts and floral Jaipuri bedsheets are the retail default in listings surfaced by search (UNVERIFIED beyond marketplace pages).

### Kitchen
- Floor: in-situ marble-chip mosaic, "a smooth, cool, speckled surface of marble chips set in cement and ground flat", the default in houses built "between the 1940s and the 1980s", divided into panels by "glass, brass, aluminium or PVC divider strips", "genuinely cool underfoot" [5]. Twitter users in 2021 called it the staple "before marble, tiles, and wood replaced it", "in the fashion till early 90s", good in kitchens because "a little bit of water spill doesn't make it dangerously slippery" [7]. Kota stone: "greenish-blue and brown" limestone from Ramganj Mandi, Rajasthan, polished, prone to flaking, "used throughout India, especially in government offices and institutions" [6][8]. Other floors named for Indian homes: red-oxide/IPS cement, Cuddapah black, Athangudi cement tiles, terracotta, marble, granite [8]. Vitrified tiles in 2000s-onward flats: UNVERIFIED (no page fetched names them as the replacement).
- Walls: distemper, "chalk, lime, pigments, and water-based binders", "soft, classic finish with a gentle matte appearance", chalks and rubs off, "part of Indian homes for generations", cheaper than emulsion [9]. Colour words from paint-company copy: warm whites, creams, light yellows, pale greens (search summary, UNVERIFIED as a specific fetched claim).
- Counter and shelves: stainless steel everywhere, thalis, katoris, dabbas, masala boxes, tumblers, ladles, water filters [13]; steel containers "inscribed with the name of the owner and date of purchase" and a grandmother's spice dabba of "little bowls holding aromatic chilli, coriander and black pepper powders, mustard and cumin seeds" [14].
- Pressure cooker: Hawkins, founded 1959 with L G Hawkins of England, "inside-fitting lid" that cannot open under pressure, over 110 million sold, brands Hawkins, Futura (hard-anodised), Contura [15]. Prestige as the other household name: UNVERIFIED (not fetched).
- Milton: steel tiffins, thermosteel bottles, insulated casseroles (the "hot box" of [1]), product colours like Light Green, Special Blue, Taupe, Dark Blue, Beige [16]. Founding date 1972 Mumbai appears only in search summaries: UNVERIFIED.
- Milk pouch: nothing solid found. Amul's Wikipedia page gave nothing on pouches; retail listings show Amul Taaza 500 ml and 1 L poly packs. Colour coding of pouches: UNVERIFIED.
- Water filter (the two-tier steel candle filter or a wall-mounted RO unit): steel water filters named in [13]; RO unit UNVERIFIED.

### Balcony with a grill
- Mumbai default: the balcony "is the extension between the window and a grille, and also doubles up as a rack for hanging wet clothes"; pigeons are the menace and owners net the whole opening (Scroll, 2020, search summary; the article itself returned 403, so quote is UNVERIFIED at the page level).
- Grills: mild steel "vertical bar or box patterns", painted black or enamel, need "regular repainting to prevent rust especially in coastal cities", used for safety, monsoon and clothes drying; newer builds swap to anodised aluminium sliders, "invisible" stainless wire grills and sliding mosquito mesh [10].
- Balconies are being enclosed for floor area (one owner "added five hundred square feet of living space"), and their traditional jobs are laundry drying, herbs, ventilation [19].

### Rooftop terrace
- Sintex is "the world's largest producer of plastic water tanks" [18]; the black overhead tank as the rooftop signature is UNVERIFIED at the page level (Wikipedia gives no product history). Clotheslines, TV dish, parapet: INFERENCE from balcony sources [10][19].
- Terrace as shared society common area with drying-clothes disputes: search summaries only, UNVERIFIED.

### The commute home
- Mumbai local: "Purple and White EMU" livery; since 2010 the front is painted yellow so track workers can see it; first class marked by "red and yellow slant stripes", ladies second class by "green and yellow slant stripes"; "no ventilating system", open doors and windows because trains "stop for only 10 seconds"; "wired grill" on windows against snatching; first class cushioned, others plastic [22]. The 2019 Uttam rake: second-class "FRP seats with a wooden finish", "LED lighting and brushless DC fans", "corrugated FRP ceiling", "modular" luggage racks, wider grab rails, stainless-steel body, "camouflaged aluminium moulded strips on floor" [23]. Newest 12-car rake: automatic sliding doors, "stainless steel seating" in second class, wider windows [24]. Commuters stand "one hand on the pole, bags held against the stomach", clustering at the footboard [25]. Siemens rakes from 2007, Bombardier from 2015, AC local from Dec 2017 [26].
- Delhi Metro: stainless-steel, 3.2 m wide coaches, fully air-conditioned, Rotem/BEML/Bombardier builds, lines named by colour [27]. The one visual constant on record is the women's coach: "bright pink seats labelled 'Ladies only'" and pink signage [28]. General seat colour: nothing solid found; the refurbishment article names new seats and paint but no colours [29]. Grey-blue longitudinal benches are UNVERIFIED.
- Auto rickshaw: "sheet-metal body resting on three wheels with a canvas roof and drop-down side curtains", handlebar steering, three passengers behind; Delhi CNG autos wear "a green and yellow livery", Mumbai keeps black-and-yellow with only a "CNG" print [30]. Bajaj, Piaggio Ape, TVS as makers [30].
- Cab: Maruti Suzuki Dzire, Toyota Etios, WagonR-class cars run as taxis; commercial cars carry yellow number plates; Delhi cabs are CNG or electric only; old kaali-peeli is "bottom half painted black and upper half painted yellow" [31]. White Dzire with beaded seat cover: nothing solid found (UNVERIFIED as a specific claim; the white body and beaded cover are widely seen).
- Bus: BEST Mumbai "historic red buses", double-deckers since 1937, electric double-decker unveiled Aug 2022 [33]. DTC Delhi colour code: red = AC low-floor with "reading lights", green = non-AC "seats, lights, and fans", orange = DIMTS cluster CNG, blue = electric; women's seats "marked with pink stickers" [34].
- Two-wheeler pillion: Honda Activa, launched 2001, India's best-selling two-wheeler by 2014, 57 percent scooter share by 2018, metal body, 110 cc CVT, colours like Gold Metallic, Black and dual tones [32]. Pillion grab rail and wide seat: search summary, UNVERIFIED.

### Kirana shop shutter at night
- Kirana = "family-owned stores that operate in fixed locations and carry both basic food and non-food items", "mom-and-pop" retail [35]. A trade blog gives the sensory set: "the scrape of a metal shutter being pulled up before sunrise", "jute sacks filled with rice and dal", "packets of shampoo hanging from a nail like tiny silver flags", a weighing scale on the counter, the "udhaar ki bahi" credit ledger [36].
- Streetlight colour: Delhi PWD is replacing 92,163 conventional lights, with 44,000 sodium-vapour lamps swapped for LED in 2025 "before Diwali" [37]. INFERENCE: a 2020s metro night is mixed, cool-white LED on main roads, residual orange sodium in lanes. Painted signboards and hand lettering on shutters: nothing solid found (the street-photography blog fetched did not describe them).
- Paan stall, street dogs, parked two-wheelers: no source fetched; UNVERIFIED as text, universal in imagery.

### Gym
- Small-town and neighbourhood gyms (ThePrint, March 2026): "bright red benches and weight machines packed into a dark room lined with mirrors"; "posters of bulky men lifting heavy weights are pasted across the room" with Hindi slogans such as "Darr ke aage dumbbell hai"; "thumping music"; fees Rs 800 to 1,200 a month; ordinary clothes, "baggy cream-coloured T-shirt, maroon trousers", "no concept of trendy gym wear" [21].

### Monsoon rain at a window
- "Sounds of the thunderous skies opening up", "muddy rivulets", "smells of fried bhajiyas and hot pakoras", clothes on clotheslines, a 60-flat building where neighbours' lives leak in [20]. Rain "will find its way in sideways" even under a covered balcony, hence PVC blinds (Houzz search summary, UNVERIFIED). Grill plus mesh at the window [10].

### The day before a festival
- Diwali: Dhanteras two days before, "householders illuminating their homes" with lamps; homes historically "whitewashed"; rangoli, jhalars, "diyas (oil lamps), candles and lanterns", new clothes [42]. Practical checklist: wash curtains, bedsheets and cushion covers, clean fans and windows, polish metal, paint or whitewash, clear the entrance for rangoli, string lights [43]. Marigold-and-mango-leaf toran at the door: search summaries only, UNVERIFIED at page level.
- Quick-commerce bags follow the same calendar (Diwali, Christmas, New Year specials) [38][39].

### The objects of ordering
- Phone, wallet, keys, cloth bag: no source specific to these; the cloth or jute bag is INFERENCE from kirana sacks [36].
- Quick-commerce bags, from the one design-analysis page fetched (Dec 2024): all three use "illustrated 2-colour" printed carry bags; Blinkit prints an "annual list of holidays" one side and a "monthly calendar" on the back with the tagline "India's last minute app"; Zepto prints festival comics ("how different cultures across India celebrate Diwali differently") with a monthly calendar; Instamart prints topical scenes (Christmas, Santa, gifts) and product-range creatives [38]. ## What separates a convincing result from a generic one

1. Floors carry the era. A Western flat has oak or grey tile; an Indian middle-class flat built before the 2000s has speckled mosaic with brass lines or kota stone, and a 2010s flat has large cream vitrified tiles (that last one UNVERIFIED). Naming the floor does more than naming the furniture [5][6][7].
2. Walls are matte and chalky, not eggshell; distemper reads flat and slightly worn at hand height [9].
3. Steel and moulded plastic, not wood and upholstery: the almirah, the dabbas, the Nilkamal chair, the Hawkins cooker [1][12][13][15].
4. Windows have grills. Every window and balcony carries a black mild-steel grill and often a pigeon net; a window without one looks foreign [10][19].
5. Light sources are mixed: tube light or LED batten on the ceiling, a coloured zero-watt bulb at night (UNVERIFIED page), a phone screen; ceiling fan visible in frame (UNVERIFIED as text, ubiquitous in imagery).
6. The commute is colour-coded by city: purple-white local with yellow front and open doors in Mumbai; pink women's-coach seats in Delhi Metro; black-and-yellow auto in Mumbai vs green-and-yellow in Delhi; red BEST vs green or red DTC [22][28][30][33][34].
7. The street at night is a mix of sodium orange and LED white, with a kirana's half-down shutter, jute sacks and sachet strings [36][37].
8. The delivery object is a printed brown paper bag with two-colour illustration and a calendar, carried by a rider in a coloured t-shirt, not a branded plastic tote [38][39][40].
9. Layering, not staging: old canon under new (a 1990s almirah, a 2020s phone, a 2024 paper bag) is the actual look; a matched set reads like a showroom or the enclosed "Four Seasons" society flat Searle describes [19].
10. Textiles are printed cotton (block-print razai, floral bedsheets, a check gamcha), not solid linen [17] (bedsheets UNVERIFIED beyond listings).
