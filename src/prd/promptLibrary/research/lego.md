# LEGO: research dossier
_Researched 2026-09-04. Sources numbered at the bottom; cite by number inline. UNVERIFIED = not on any fetched page. Two LEGO Group primary documents anchor this: the 2013 LEGO Brand Manual minifigure drawing [24] and the 2016 LEGO Moulding Colour Palette [6]. Trademark note: the LEGO Group says the word must be used only as an adjective ("LEGO bricks"), never as a noun, and the 2x4 brick and the minifigure are themselves trademarks [38][39]; our library strips branding anyway._

## Brick geometry
| FACT | value | source # |
|---|---|---|
| Base unit / module | all dimensions are multiples of 1.6 mm; 5 units = one 8 mm module (a "stud's worth") [16]; the LEGO Group's own word for the 1x1 footprint is "module" [36] | 16, 36 |
| Stud pitch (centre to centre) | 8 mm nominal; measured 7.985 mm on Lugnet [17]; drawing shows 8 [14] | 14, 16, 17 |
| Stud diameter | 4.8 mm (3 units) [16]; drawing Ø4.8 [14]; LDraw 12 LDU [3] | 3, 14, 16 |
| Stud height | 1.7 mm in section, 1.8 mm on the elevation of the same drawing [14]; Brick Owl 1.7 mm [18]; "about 1 unit, depending on the era" [16]; LDraw 4 LDU = 1.6 mm [3]. Say "about 1.7 mm" | 3, 14, 16, 18 |
| Brick height (no stud) | 9.6 mm = 6 units [16]; drawing 9.6 [14]; 24 LDU [3] | 3, 14, 16 |
| Plate height | 3.2 mm = 2 units [16]; 8 LDU [3]; "3 of them on top of each other, you get a brick" [33] | 3, 16, 33 |
| 5:6 ratio | "ratio of the height of standard beams, to the width of studs is 5:6" [17]; Zoe Blade: 5-unit width vs 6-unit height [16]; SNOT rule: 2 studs wide = 5 plates tall, 6 studs wide = 5 bricks tall [32] | 16, 17, 32 |
| 2x4 brick envelope | 31.8 x 15.8 x 9.6 mm [14]; each side is 8 mm per stud minus 0.2 mm [18], i.e. 0.1 mm clearance per side [16]; "0.2 mm gap between bricks next to each other" [15] | 14, 15, 16, 18 |
| Wall thickness | 1.2 mm on the 2x4 drawing [14] (Zoe Blade generalises to 1 unit = 1.6 mm [16]); top "ceiling" 1 mm [14] | 14, 16 |
| Tube under the brick | outer Ø6.51 mm, inner Ø4.8 mm (it grips four studs); pins under 1xN bricks Ø2.6 mm [14]; Orionrobots gives 6.31 [17]: use Bartneck's measured 6.51 | 14, 17 |
| Stud and tube principle | patent filed 28 Jan 1958 at 1:58 pm; studs + tubes create "clutch power"; six 2x4 bricks combine 915,103,765 ways [19] | 19 |
| Mould precision | "accuracy of 1/200 mm" [20]; machines "tolerances as small as 10 micrometres" [1] | 1, 20 |
| LDU (LDraw unit) | 1 LDU = 0.4 mm; brick width 20 LDU, brick height 24, plate 8, stud diameter 12, stud height 4 [3][45] | 3, 45 |
| Tile vs plate | tile = "same height as a plate, but no studs on top", used as a smooth finish [33][34] | 33, 34 |
| Slope angles that exist | 10, 13, 15, 18, 25, 30, 33, 45, 55, 65, 75 degrees; 45 is "the most common angle" (2x slopes), 33 "in the popular 3x slope bricks"; 65 = 2x1x2 slope 60481; 75 = 2x1x3 slope 4460 [37] | 37 |
| Cheese slope | 30 degree 1x1x2/3 slope, part 54200 [37]; BrickLink calls it "Slope 30 1 x 1 x 2/3" (UNVERIFIED, search snippet only). SEALUG says 33 degrees [34] and Bricks McGee "approximately 45" [35]: both wrong against the part catalogue | 34, 35, 37 |
| Bar / clip standard | 3.2 mm bar diameter [18] (community calls it the 3.18 bar; minifigure hand grips it, see below) | 18, 24 |
| Round bricks | exist (Brick Architect and glossaries) but no fetched page gives their dimensions: UNVERIFIED beyond "1x1 round plate is nicknamed Apollo stud / Polo" [36] | 36 |
| Stud logo | real studs carry the LEGO logo: BrickLink Studio's renderer has a "Stud logo" switch that "defines whether you want to see the LEGO logos on the parts" [29]; BrickNerd's 2025 April-fools piece is built on the real premise of "the raised LEGO logo on studs" [31]. So the prompt can legitimately say "plain studs" | 29, 31 |

## Minifigure anatomy
| FACT | value | source # |
|---|---|---|
| Height | "exactly the same height as four LEGO bricks fitted together" (LEGO Group) [22]; 4 bricks = 3.84 cm without the head stud, "exactly 4 cm" with it [27]; 25 plates incl. stud [26]. Brand Manual: stud 1.8 + head 9.6 + body 28.8 = 40.2 mm [24] | 22, 24, 26, 27 |
| Head | cylinder with a stud on top [2]; Brand Manual: 10.2 mm wide, 8.5 mm face height (9.6 incl. neck collar), 11.2 mm over the widest point, top stud 1.8 mm tall, eye centres 4.01 mm apart [24]; Kyle Swan drawing: 10.2 wide, 8.5 main height, 11.65 total [25] | 2, 24, 25 |
| Classic face | "two solid black eye dots and black painted smile" (LEGO Group) [22]; only face used until 1989 [28]; printed, not moulded [2] | 2, 22, 28 |
| Parts | head, torso, arms, hands, hips, legs [2]; "usually consist of ten parts" [28] (2 arms, 2 hands, 2 legs) | 2, 28 |
| Articulation | "seven points of articulation: swivel head, swivel arms, swivel wrists, and swivel legs"; legs rotate about 90 degrees forward [2] | 2 |
| Torso | trapezoid: 10.59 mm at the top, 15.43 mm at the bottom, 12.82 mm tall [25]; "same width and depth as a 1x2 LEGO brick" [26]; 2 studs wide not counting arms [27]; printing goes on the torso front [2] | 2, 25, 26 |
| Arms and hands | hands "resemble the letter C" and their tops are "the same size as the studs" [2]; Brand Manual marks the hand ring *C4.9 (outer) and *C3.2 (grip), the footnote says "C: circumference" but the values match stud diameter 4.8 and bar 3.2 [24]; Swan: hand outer 5.1, inner 3.1, height 4.4 [25] | 2, 24, 25 |
| Hips and legs | hip width 15.4 mm, each leg 8 mm wide, legs 13.8 mm tall, foot 3.2 mm (one plate), leg depth 7.8 mm [24]; Swan: leg 14.87, foot 3.22, waist 14.14 x 2.19 [25] | 24, 25 |
| Whole-body brackets | shoulders to feet 27.7 mm, torso+legs 28.8, arm-top to foot 25.1 [24] | 24 |
| Hair and hats | sit on the head stud (a stud "allowing piece attachment") [28]; the astronaut helmet "had to work in space, castle and town" (Knudsen) [23] | 23, 28 |
| Proportions statement | "a rather cartoonish representation of a human being with the overall proportions being squatter than that of an average human" [27]; scale about 1:42 [26][27] | 26, 27 |
| Skin tones | yellow "chosen based on focus group feedback in the early and mid-1970s" [22]; first natural skin tones 2003 (Lando Calrissian, NBA players) [22][28], expanded to licensed lines 2004 [28]; the LEGO colours are Nougat 18, Light Nougat 283, Medium Nougat 312 [5][6] | 5, 6, 22, 28 |
| History | launched 1978 [22][23]; 1975 "stage extra" precursor, solid torso, no face [22]; designer Jens Nygaard Knudsen [2]; "more than 650 unique faces", 8,000+ characters [22] | 2, 22, 23 |

## Material, finish and the official render look
- ABS "since 1963" for "most LEGO pieces"; it gives "the unique connective grip (clutch power), high gloss and colors LEGO bricks are known for" (LEGO Group) [21]; moulding switched from cellulose acetate to "color-fast ABS" [20]; ABS heated to 232 C [1].
- Other LEGO materials: HIPS for baseplates, arMABS for transparent elements (recycled artificial-marble worktops), PC hinges, bio-PE from sugarcane for flexible parts, SEBS tyres [4]. So bricks are glossy opaque ABS, baseplates a different plastic.
- Mould seams and injection (gate) points: nothing solid found on a fetched page. BrickLink Studio's renderer offers optional "Scratches" (random scratches and bite marks) and "UV Degradation" (yellowing) effects, off unless chosen [29]: an official-style render is clean and unscratched.
- Stud logo: renderable or not via the "Stud logo" switch [29]; our clause says plain studs.
- BrickLink Studio (Photoreal / Eyesight, built on Blender's Cycles): "your design will always cast a shadow on the Ground", ground snaps to the lowest part; background is "a solid background color or a transparent background"; lighting presets Building, Mechanic, Asteroid, Dawn, Piazza; perspective or orthographic camera; viewport angle is the render default; rubber parts render matte [29][30].
- Mecabricks: "Build & Render your 3D LEGO models online", in-browser [46]; its Blender add-on adds bevels, scratches, fingerprints, and sticker detection (search snippets only: UNVERIFIED).
- lego.com product image conventions (white or light-grey ground, three-quarter view, studs up, figures standing free rather than on a baseplate): nothing solid found on a fetched page; treat as inference from the Studio defaults above (shadow on a ground plane, solid background). Gemini's own product-shot template is "studio-lit ... three-point softbox ... slightly elevated 45-degree" [41].

## Building vocabulary
| TERM | meaning | why a model responds | source # |
|---|---|---|---|
| brick-built | "a creation which is made of bricks as opposed to using molded pieces" | tells the model the object is assembled from many small bricks, not one smooth toy | 34, 33 |
| minifig scale | a build "for the scale of standard LEGO minifigures", about 1:42 | fixes scale so doors, cars, chairs read right against a 4 cm figure | 34, 26 |
| SNOT | "Studs Not On Top": elements used sideways or upside down for smooth or sculpted surfaces | permits studless side faces without breaking the brick look | 32, 34 |
| studs up | conventional orientation, studs facing up on every top face | the single strongest "this is a brick model" cue; our default | 33 (studs defined), inference |
| greebling | "small, non-functioning part added ... to make it visually interesting" | surface detail texture, use sparingly | 34, 36 |
| tile | plate-height piece "with no studs on top", a smooth finish | says "smooth here", e.g. floors, screens | 33, 34 |
| plate | 3.2 mm thin piece, three make a brick | thin layers, steps, colour bands | 33 |
| baseplate | large flat foundation piece, no anti-studs underneath | a ground that is itself studded | 33 |
| jumper plate | 1x2 or 2x2 plate with one centred stud, for half-stud offset | centring details | 34 |
| cheese slope | 30 degree 1x1x2/3 slope (part 54200) | tiny wedge details, low roofs | 37 |
| 1x1 round plate | nicknamed "Apollo stud" or "Polo" | dots, lights, buttons | 36 |
| element / Element ID / Design ID | LEGO Group terms: an element is unique in colour AND shape (Element ID); Design ID is the shape only | use "elements" in prompt copy, it is LEGO's own word | 36, 33 |
| System | LEGO Group's line of "building elements, minifigures and sets"; from "System i Leg" | the compatibility promise | 33, 36 |
| module | LEGO Group term for the width and depth of a 1x1x1 brick | the 8 mm grid | 36 |
| building instructions | LEGO's phrase for the step-by-step booklet (used verbatim on lego.com) | : | 38 |

## Model-side levers
- OpenAI image API: "All prompts and generated images are filtered in accordance with our content policy"; `moderation` parameter `auto` (default) or `low`; blocks return `moderation_blocked` with categories (harassment, self-harm, sexual, violence) [40]. No fetched OpenAI page names trademarks or brand words; the usage-policy page returned 403 twice. Whether "LEGO" is refused: UNVERIFIED (nothing solid found).
- Google Gemini image docs: "Make sure you have the necessary rights ... Don't generate content that infringe on others' rights" [41]; the Imagen guide even suggests "your company logo in different materials" and recommends "made of [material]" and "in the shape of [object]" phrasing plus camera words (35mm, macro, studio photo) [42]; the Prohibited Use Policy bans violating "intellectual property rights" [43]. No explicit brand-name rule.
- LEGO Group side: the LEGO name may be used only as an adjective, "LEGO® is a trademark of the LEGO Group ... which does not sponsor, authorize or endorse", logo never [39]; the 2x4 brick and the minifigure are protected trademarks for commercial use [38]. Reason enough for our library to say "brick-built" and "minifigure" without the brand word.
- Community terms: one fetched prompt guide recommends adding "plastic toy material", yellow/tan skin, "authentic proportions", "studio lighting" [44]. Claims that "brick-built", "minifigure", "studs" reliably trigger the look, or that Midjourney refuses "LEGO": UNVERIFIED (search snippets only).
- Gemini's own product-shot template ("studio-lit product photograph ... three-point softbox ... slightly elevated 45-degree") is the fetched recipe for the look [41].

## Proposed clauses
(a) OBJECT constant (each clause with source):
"Rebuild the subject as a brick-built model of interlocking plastic building elements [34][36]: studs up on every top face [33], round studs 4.8 mm across on an 8 mm grid, about 1.7 mm tall, plain with no lettering [14][16][29]; bricks 9.6 mm tall, plates one third of that, tiles for smooth surfaces [16][33]; curves stepped in 45 and 33 degree slopes, small 30 degree wedges [37]; glossy opaque ABS, clean, unscratched, no yellowing [21][29]; only the official palette: Bright Red 21, Bright Blue 23, Bright Yellow 24, White 1, Black 26, Medium Stone Grey 194, Dark Stone Grey 199, Brick Yellow 5 [5][6]; standing on a plain ground plane with a soft contact shadow, solid neutral background [29]; studio-lit, three-point softbox, slightly elevated 45 degree three-quarter view [41]."

(b) MINIFIGURE constant:
"Render the person as a minifigure: exactly four bricks tall, 4 cm with the head stud [22][27]; cylindrical head 10.2 mm wide with a plain stud on top [24]; printed face, two black dot eyes and a black curved smile [22]; trapezoid torso 2 studs wide, narrower at the shoulders [25][26]; straight cylindrical arms, C-shaped claw hands the width of a stud [2][24]; hip block 15.4 mm wide, two blocky legs 8 mm wide with one-plate feet [24]; seven joints only: neck, two shoulders, two wrists, two hips [2]; hair or hat as a separate moulded piece seated on the head stud [28]; Bright Yellow 24 skin for a generic figure, Nougat 18 / Light Nougat 283 / Medium Nougat 312 for a likeness [5][6][22]; outfit as flat torso printing, not moulded [2]; glossy ABS, ground shadow, no baseplate [21][29]."

(c) Colour-substitution table: see "Official colours" above (our word -> LEGO name + ID). No-match rows: mustard, brass, tortoiseshell; cream and deep-purple are nearest-only.

(d) Compact twins:

[5][6][14][16][21][29][33][37][41]
