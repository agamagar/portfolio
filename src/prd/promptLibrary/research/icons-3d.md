# 3D icons (flat to soft 3D) - research dossier
_Researched 2026-09-20. Sources listed at the bottom, numbered; cite by number inline._

Target: any flat 2D icon becomes a soft, rounded, matte 3D icon in the register of Microsoft Fluent 3D emoji, Apple macOS 11 (Big Sur) app icons and the Dribbble/Behance "3D icon" packs. The reference image is a deep violet rounded-square calendar body, two darker binder rings on top, a pale lavender heart in front; matte, slightly soft plastic or clay; broad soft studio light from upper-left; faint darker gradient toward the lower edge; soft diffuse drop shadow on pure white; seen straight on, depth read only from the filleted edges, the raised heart and the rings' shading. No text.

Reading rule: UNVERIFIED = not on any fetched page. Inference is marked as inference. Where a question turned up nothing, the section says "nothing solid found" rather than filling the gap.

## What the medium actually is

**It is a 3D render of a flat icon, not a 3D object.** The two canonical references were both built exactly this way. Microsoft's Fluent emoji were "initially created as two-dimensional designs, and later modeled and animated in 3D" [4]; Tendril, the studio that built the 3D set, "started with 2D illustrations using simple base shapes as a construction logic for all our Emojis before diving into 3D" [5]. Their lead modeller said the form target outright: "volume modeling was perfect for the look we aimed for: rounded models, soft curves and bevels" [6]. The Fluent flat, colour and 3D styles share one drawing; the 3D style adds only volume, material and light.

**Apple's macOS 11 icon language is the other anchor, and it is a set of fixed camera and light rules, not a style.** Apple's own words: "app icons share a common set of visual attributes, including the rounded-rectangle shape, front-facing perspective, level position, and uniform drop shadow. Rooted in the macOS 11 design language, these attributes showcase the lifelike rendering style" [1]. A tool may "float just above the background and extend slightly past the icon boundaries", but must stay "visually unified with the background" and not "overwhelm the rounded-rectangle shape" [1]. And the light: "Shadows and highlights should suggest a light source that faces the icon, positioned just above center and tilted slightly downward" [1]. That is the front-elevation, one-light, one-shadow grammar our reference image uses. Note: Apple's light is above-centre; our reference and Google's material icon lighting are upper-left (next section). Also note the CURRENT Apple HIG (2026, Liquid Glass) tells app developers the opposite for submission assets ("there's no need to include specular highlights, drop shadows between layers, beveled edges") because the system now renders those effects live [2]; that page is about what to hand the OS, not how a rendered icon looks, so cite [1] for the look and [2] only for the layer logic.

**Google's 2014 material product-icon spec is the most explicit written recipe for this exact lighting, and it is one hue graded by light.** "Each icon is cut, folded, and lit as paper would be ... The matte-like finish interacts with light through subtle highlights and consistent shadows" [22]. The anatomy is five named things: a "Tinted edge" (top edge, "a mixture of a color with white"), a "Shaded edge" (bottom edge, "a mixture of a color with a darker hue"), a "Contact shadow" ("A soft shadow around all edges of a raised material element"), a "Finish" ("A soft tint above all elements to provide surface lighting, fading from upper-left to lower-right") and the drop shadow of "A material element raised above, and casting a shadow upon, the material background" [22]. The light itself: "the top light from above casts a soft shadow surrounding an element lightly on the top and left. The shadow is slightly heavier below and to the right" and "The finish layer is a result of the virtual 45º light source. It extends from the top-left corner" [22]. Every value they give is small: shadow opacity 20%, Y offset 4dp, blur 4dp; edge tint and shade 1dp at 20%; finish gradient 10% to 0% [22]. That is the "faint darker gradient toward the lower edge" of our reference, written as numbers.

**In the render tools the look is three decisions: fillet, matte dielectric, big soft light.** Blender's Bevel modifier "bevels the edges of the mesh", with Width as "the size of the bevel effect", Segments as "the number of edge loops added along the bevel's face" (1 segment is a chamfer, more make it round) and a Profile shape "from concave to convex" that "has no effect if Segments is less than 2" [10]. Cinema 4D's Extrude caps say the same thing in its own words: a "Round" transition whose "radius can be defined using the Shape Depth setting", where "A value of 1 will always produce a straight chamfer" [15]. Material is a Principled BSDF with high Roughness: "A value of 0.0 gives a perfectly sharp reflection, while 1.0 gives a diffuse reflection" [11]; Spline's PBR doc puts it in plain words: "Low is a mirror or polished metal; high is chalk, rubber or unfinished wood" [17]. Light is an area light: "The area light simulates light originating from a surface (or surface-like) emitter" and "produces shadows with soft borders"; "Lights with larger size have softer shadows and specular highlights" [12].

**What the Fluent 3D emoji are NOT: matte.** Emojipedia describes the shipped 3D set as "glossy, gradient-heavy versions" of the flat Fluent designs [8]. So Fluent 3D is our FORM reference (simple base shapes, "imperfect circle", soft curves and bevels [4][5][6]) but not our MATERIAL reference. Our reference image's clay-matte finish is closer to Google's "matte-like finish" [22] and to the Dribbble "clay" packs; the prompt must say so explicitly or the model will drift to Fluent's gloss.

## What separates a convincing result from a generic one

1. **Depth comes from stacked slabs, not from perspective.** The convincing icon is a front elevation whose depth is read from three cues only: the rolled edge, the raised element and its contact shadow, and the ground shadow. Apple mandates "front-facing perspective, level position" [1]; the 3dicons library kept "Front" as a fixed camera with its own light set [9]. The generic result tilts the camera because most 3D captions are three-quarter views. Say the view.

2. **The base shape's corner is a fillet in 3D and a squircle in plan.** Two different roundings, both needed. In plan the corner is the Apple squircle ("quintic superellipse" [3]); in section the edge is a rounded bevel with several segments, not a chamfer [10][15]. A single "rounded" in the prompt gets you one or the other.

3. **Matte is a decision, not a default, because the nearest famous reference is glossy.** Fluent 3D is "glossy, gradient-heavy" [8]. Google's product-icon material is "matte-like" with "subtle highlights" [22]. The soft look is high roughness [11][17], a little subsurface [11][28], and a large light so the highlight is broad and dim rather than a hot dot [12][26]. The practitioner thread is blunt that material alone is not enough: "the second thing you should work on is the lighting, that is what gives the appeal to that 'style'" [28].

4. **Every shadow is small and soft, and there are exactly three.** Contact shadow where parts meet [22][23], a slightly heavier shadow below and to the right of a raised element [22], and one diffuse ground shadow [1]. Google's numbers (20% opacity, 4dp offset, 4dp blur, 1dp edges) are the register [22]. Hard shadows come from small lights; "Lights with larger size have softer shadows" [12]. The generic render has a black pool under the icon and a hard cast shadow across the body.

5. **One hue, graded by light.** The reference is one violet at three values. Google's system does this by construction: tint on the top edge, shade on the bottom edge, a 10% finish gradient from upper-left [22]. The 3dicons author found colour drift across angles the hardest part: "Managing the same color for all angles is a challenge. I tried and failed a few times before finally fixing the colors and gradients for all icons" [9]. Inference from that: hold the base colour to the flat icon's own hex and let light make the ramp; do not ask the model for "a palette". Whether the Dribbble packs use a strict one-hue ramp rule: nothing solid found on any fetched page; the one-hue rule here rests on Google's spec [22] and the reference image itself.

6. **The metaphor survives only if the silhouette does.** Apple: "Embrace simplicity" and "Establish a single focus point" [1]; Fluent's whole system is "simple geometry" with parts "softened, curved, and bent" [4]. The generic failure is the icon turning into a product shot: a real desk calendar with paper pages, or a heart with a scene behind it. Apple's own instruction to "Make real objects look real" [1] is for detailed macOS tool icons and is the wrong register here; our reference stays a symbol made of clay.

7. **Consistency across a set is a grid and a light rig, not taste.** Google: "Keyline shapes are the foundation of the grid ... maintain a consistent visual proportion across related product icons" [22]; Apple: "use the icon grid to ensure that centered inner elements like circles use a size that's consistent with other icons in the system" and "Keep primary content within the icon grid bounding box" [1]. The 3dicons library reused "three sets of the same lights" across every icon [9]. Padding inside the frame: nothing solid found as a number for 3D icon packs; the only numeric padding on a fetched page is Material's flat-icon live area, which is a different medium.

8. **Puffy or inflated is a different treatment, and no primary page documents it for icons.** The Blender Artists thread on inflating a flat icon offers "cloth simulation and internal pressure" then a boolean to flatten one side [29]; that is forum advice, not a documented workflow. Tendril's "rounded models, soft curves and bevels" [6] is bevel-and-volume modelling, not inflation. UNVERIFIED that any of the reference packs use inflation; our reference reads as filleted slabs, so the prompt should say "filleted" not "puffy".

## Model-side levers

Stub; the per-model dossier (research/models.md) owns this. Two notes specific to this system: (a) the flat source icon goes in as the reference image and the prompt must forbid re-drawing it (silhouette, proportions and hue are conserved; only volume, material and light are added); (b) "transparent background" is a known trap in this library (models paint a checkerboard), so the ground is "pure white" with the shadow painted on it, never "no background".

## Proposed clause upgrades

Each clause is a sentence that can ship as-is; rationale and source follow.

1. **View clause.** "Front elevation, camera level with the icon, no perspective tilt; depth reads only from the rounded edges, the raised parts and their shadows."
   Rationale: fixes the three-quarter drift; Apple's "front-facing perspective, level position" [1]; 3dicons' fixed Front camera [9].

2. **Base-shape clause.** "The body is a squircle in outline (Apple app-icon corner) and its edges are filleted with a smooth rounded bevel of several segments, about a twentieth of the body width; no chamfers, no sharp edges."
   Rationale: two roundings named separately [3][10][15]; the width number is the vendor "macro bevel 2-5%" range [16], labelled secondary.

3. **Stacking clause.** "Each shape of the flat icon is its own slab: the body lowest, the rings and the heart raised in front of it, each with the same filleted edge, with a soft contact shadow where they meet."
   Rationale: Apple's float-above rule [1]; Google's "Elevate" pattern and "Contact shadow" [22]; AO definition [23].

4. **Material clause.** "Matte soft-touch plastic, like clay: high roughness, no mirror reflections, a faint velvety sheen near the edges and a little subsurface softness; NOT glossy, NOT the shiny Fluent emoji finish."
   Rationale: roughness 1.0 "gives a diffuse reflection" [11]; "chalk, rubber" [17]; sheen and SSS definitions [11]; clay thread [28]; explicit negative because the famous reference is glossy [8].

5. **Light clause.** "One large softbox (area light) from the upper-left, high and in front of the icon, with a strong fill so the shadow side stays the icon's colour; highlights are broad and dim; no rim light unless the icon is dark."
   Rationale: area light "produces shadows with soft borders", larger size means softer shadows and highlights [12]; softbox size rule [26]; fill purpose [24]; upper-left matches Google's 45 degree top-left finish [22].

6. **Shadow clause.** "Three shadows only, all soft and low opacity: a contact shadow in the seams, a slightly heavier shadow just below and to the right of each raised part, and one diffuse drop shadow on the pure white ground under the whole icon."
   Rationale: Google's shadow spec (20%, 4dp offset, 4dp blur, "heavier below and to the right") [22]; Apple's "uniform drop shadow" [1]; shadow catcher is the tool word for a white ground that only receives shadow [14].

7. **Colour clause.** "One hue from the flat icon, graded by light: the body at the icon's own colour, sunk parts a shade darker, the raised part a tint lighter, and a faint finish gradient from upper-left to lower-right on the body; no new hues."
   Rationale: tint and shade definitions and the 10% finish [22]; the colour-consistency failure in 3dicons [9]; Fluent's "relatively strict color palette" [4].

8. **Conservation clause.** "Keep the flat icon's silhouette, proportions and metaphor exactly; add volume, material and light and nothing else: no scene, no props, no text, no real-world detail such as paper pages or hinges."
   Rationale: "Embrace simplicity", "single focus point", text "doesn't support accessibility or localization" [1]; Fluent's 2D-first construction [4][5]; the failure mode is Apple's "Make real objects look real" register [1], which is the wrong one here.

9. **Set clause (when generating more than one).** "Every icon in the set uses the same camera, the same light rig, the same edge radius and the same ground shadow, and sits on the same keyline grid with the same margin inside the frame."
   Rationale: keyline shapes for "consistent visual proportion" [22]; Apple's icon grid [1]; 3dicons' reused light sets [9]. Margin value: nothing solid found; leave it as "same margin" until measured.

10. **Anti-clause (what not to write).** Do not write "puffy" or "inflated" (undocumented for this look, and it changes the form [29]); do not write "bevelled" alone (chamfer risk [10][15]); do not write "3D emoji style" without "matte" (gloss [8]); do not write "transparent background" (checkerboard trap, see Model-side levers).
