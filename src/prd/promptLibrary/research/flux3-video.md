# FLUX.3 video: research dossier
_Researched 2026-10-02. Sources listed at the bottom, numbered; cite by number inline._

Scope: prompting Black Forest Labs' FLUX 3 Video image-to-video, with a designed still (real paneer pizza photo set inside a flat graphic layout: lace doily or jharokha arch, blue-pottery tiles, soft-pixel folk motifs, paper grain) as the first frame. The food moves; the graphic layout holds.

## What the medium actually is

**The model.** "FLUX.3" is FLUX 3, BFL's first video model. Announced 23 July 2026 as a multimodal flow model trained jointly on images, video and audio, with variants FLUX 3 Video, FLUX 3 Image, FLUX 3 Action and a planned open-weight FLUX 3 Dev [1][2]. "FLUX 3 Video, Part 1: Generation" (4 August 2026) made video generation from text and images generally available through the BFL API and partners, at HD (720p) and Full HD (1080p), up to 20 s, with synchronized audio, multi-shot in one generation, and a Draft Mode [1][3]. Evaluations were called "preliminary" with improvements expected during early access [2]. A video upscaler (2K, 4K) followed on 20 August 2026 [1].

**Modes in the API** (`/v1/flux-3-video`) [5][6]:
- `t2v` text only; `i2v` with `keyframes`; `v2v` continuation from a `start_video`; `draft_enhance` to re-render a chosen draft at full quality.
- `i2v` keyframes: one image is the opening frame, two pin start and end, up to ten can be pinned to timestamps [5][6].
- A separate tool, FLUX Video Edit [fast], edits an existing clip by instruction [5][13].
- The cookbook also documents `reference_images` (1 to 10 images that steer identity but never appear on screen); it cannot be combined with `keyframes` in one request [8]. Palmier's build does not expose it (per the brief, not verified by us).

**Hard numbers** [4][5][6]: duration 5 to 20 whole seconds or `auto`; output 24 fps in every mode; resolutions `hd` (1280x704 at 16:9), `fhd` (1920x1088), `qhd`, `uhd`. The higher classes run the same generation through a video upsampler, "so the shot is identical across all four and only the delivered size differs" [5]. Aspect ratios 21:9, 2:1, 16:9, 4:3, 1:1, 3:4, 9:16, 9:21, or `auto` (chosen from prompt and inputs) [5][6]. Audio is generated by default (`generate_audio: true`) [5][6].

**What the first frame is.** With one keyframe the still is "on screen, pixel for pixel, as the opening frame. The clip animates out of it"; BFL verified this by extracting decoded frame 0 losslessly [8]. With two keyframes both stills are on screen pixel for pixel at their pinned frames and the model generates the in-between; end-frame pinning is marked experimental, "expect more variance between runs ... re-roll a miss" [8]. The closing frame sits at `duration x 24`, so a start-plus-end clip needs an integer duration [8].

**Prompt handling.**
- Rewriting: "Your prompt is interpreted and expanded before generation" [7]. Runware's vendor doc adds that "every explicit choice you make is respected. The rewriting fills in what you leave underspecified rather than overwriting what you set" [17]. There is no user switch for this in the BFL schema (no `prompt_upsampling` field on the video request) [6].
- Prompt length: the BFL schema sets no `maxLength` on `prompt` [6]. Runware documents up to 10,000 characters but warns attention is not uniform: "Past a few hundred well-chosen words, later clauses start to fight earlier ones for focus and details drop out" [17]. Palmier's own field cap: nothing solid found.
- Negative prompts: no negative prompt field in the video schema (`additionalProperties: false`, no such property) [6]. FLUX 3 Image explicitly "has no negative prompt field" [14].
- Seeds: no seed input in the video schema [6]; Scenario confirms "No fixed seed control" [18]. The only way to keep a take is draft then `draft_enhance`, which reproduces "the original mode, prompt, seed, and conditioning media" [4][6]. Runware says draft mode currently applies only to text-to-video [17]; BFL's schema allows `draft` on `i2v` [6]. Conflict, UNVERIFIED which holds in Palmier. Whether Palmier's "720p draft" is BFL `draft: true` or just `hd` resolution: UNVERIFIED.

| rack focus | Focus shifts from one plane to another in the same shot | In BFL's focus cheatsheet; StudioBinder: directs attention without changing angle | 12, 25 |

## What separates a convincing result from a generic one

1. **Prompt the motion, not the scene.** BFL: "Re-describing what's in the frame (the lighting, the setting, the subject's look) invites the model to re-imagine it instead of animating it. Say what moves, and when several things move, give them an order" [8]. A generic prompt re-describes the pizza; a convincing one sequences steam, then hand, then pull.
2. **Name the preserved parts, by name only.** For edits BFL lists what stays element by element, "not only 'the rest of the image'" [15]; Scenario's i2v advice is to "preserve the ... design ... exactly" [18]. The two rules fit together if the hold list names the layout parts (doily, arch, tiles, motifs, paper) without describing their look. Inference: we found no BFL page that tests this on i2v.
3. **Lock the camera when anything must hold.** BFL's melt example: "keep the camera still so the transformation is the only motion" and "The candlelight and the hall never change" [8]. An unnamed camera drifts [17].
4. **Inference, load-bearing: a push-in and a static layout fight each other.** The layout lives inside the first frame's pixels, so any camera move (push-in, rack focus, tilt) moves, crops or defocuses the doily and arch with the food. If the layout must keep its exact shapes, use a locked-off camera and do the push-in as a scale keyframe in the Palmier timeline after generation, or accept that the whole printed card moves as one flat object.
5. **One clean beat per 5 to 7 s.** Runware: "A single continuous take with one small motion fits 5", a shot with a beginning and an end "wants 7 to 10", and squeezing more into 5 s makes the model "compress or drop content" [17]. Steam plus hand plus pull is two or three beats: 8 to 10 s, or split into two clips.
6. **Timestep the beats when they must land on marks.** BFL's timestep format: two or three beats for a 5 s clip [10].
7. **The pull is lit, not just stretched.** The commercial look is light passing through the strands, with the neighbouring slices staying put [19]. Steam shows only against a dark ground with backlight [21]. Inference: over a pale paper-grain layout, steam may vanish or turn into a white haze; ask for it over the darker crust and charred edge.
8. **Inference, food truth: paneer does not string.** Paneer is a non-melting fresh cheese, so a believable pull comes from melted mozzarella. Naming paneer as the source of the strands invites a rubbery or physically wrong pull. Not sourced; check against the actual pizza.
9. **Text in frame.** Quoted words in a prompt are rendered as text, and a quoted line with no visible speaker "tends to render as burned-in text" [7]. BFL's own example prompts end with "No on-screen text" [7][8]. Any lettering already printed in the layout is held only by the first frame plus a locked camera. How well FLUX 3 keeps existing in-frame text stable over 8 to 10 s: nothing solid found.

## Model-side levers

- **Keyframes = 1 (first frame only).** This is the safest path: frame 0 is guaranteed pixel-exact, and nothing guarantees the layout at later frames [8].
- **Keyframes = 2, with the end frame made by editing the start frame.** BFL's cookbook rule: make the end still "one generation plus one edit, not two generations" so that background and light "match exactly and only the subject changes"; otherwise "the video has to morph the room along with" the subject [8]. For us: make the last frame by editing the designed still (slice lifted, pull visible) so the doily, arch and tiles are identical at both ends. Experimental, so re-roll a miss [8]. Inference: this is the strongest lever we found for keeping the layout fixed, because it is pinned at both ends of the clip.
- **Same still as first and last frame** gives a loop that has to return to its start [11]. Inference: good for a steam-only cinemagraph; wrong for a lift, which would have to put the slice back.
- **Duration:** pin an integer once the beat count is known, since `auto` picks its own length [17]. For a start-plus-end clip the integer is required [8].
- **Audio:** on by default in the API [5]. Whether Palmier passes `generate_audio`: UNVERIFIED. If audio is on, describe it ("the soft crackle of the crust, a low kitchen room tone, no music") rather than leave it to chance; BFL examples state "no music" explicitly [17].
- **No seed, no negative field** [6][18]. Iterate one layer at a time: "pin that layer and leave the rest of the prompt alone" [17].
- **Length:** stay at a few hundred words or fewer [17]. BFL's i2v examples run one to two sentences; its cookbook i2v prompts run about 100 words [8][11].
- **Clause order:** BFL's cheatsheet says to start with subject and action, then add framing, movement and style [12]. Its i2v examples open with the start-frame anchor, then the action in order, then camera, then audio, then "No on-screen text" [8].
- **Camera terms:** use one framing term and one movement term; "`low aerial handheld orbit push-in` usually is not" readable [12].

## Proposed clause upgrades

Each is written to be composed; the order below is the proposed clause order.

1. **Anchor.** "The video begins exactly on the provided image and holds still for a beat."
   Rationale: BFL's own i2v phrasing, which sets up the pixel-exact first frame [8][17].

2. **Camera (default).** "Locked-off camera: the frame does not move, zoom or refocus for the whole clip."
   Rationale: an unnamed camera drifts, and any move takes the flat layout with it [17][8]. Do the push-in in the Palmier timeline instead (inference, see point 4 above).

3. **Camera (alternative, only when the card may move).** "One slow push-in toward the hero slice over the full clip, the whole printed card moving as one flat object."
   Rationale: push-in is a named reliable move; pace it with the duration [17][24]. The second half is inference, aimed at stopping the arch from bending in parallax.

4. **Hold list.** "The lace doily border, the jharokha arch, the blue-pottery tiles, the soft-pixel folk motifs and the paper grain are flat printed graphics that stay exactly as they are, same shapes, same colours, never moving."
   Rationale: name each preserved element, not "the rest" [15][18]. Names only, no restyling of them [8]. The cinemagraph idea of a frozen frame with one moving element [12].

5. **Beat order (8 to 10 s).** "First, thin wisps of steam rise and curl off the charred crust. Then a hand enters from the lower right, lifts the hero slice, and long strands of melted mozzarella stretch and sag between the slice and the pie, side light glowing through the strands, the neighbouring slices staying seated. The hand pauses at the top of the lift."
   Rationale: sequence the moving things [8]; one beat per 5 to 7 s [17]; light through the pull, other slices fixed [19]; steam over the darker crust for contrast [21]; mozzarella strands (inference 8 above).

6. **Timing variant (when beats must land on marks).** "0 to 2s: steam wisps rise off the crust. 2 to 7s: the hand lifts the slice and the cheese pull stretches. 7 to 9s: the hand holds, the strands slowly sag."
   Rationale: BFL timestep format [10].

7. **Motion character.** "The cheese pull plays out in slow motion, as if overcranked at 120 fps and played back at 24."
   Rationale: Runware's slow-motion phrasing plus the craft ratio [17][26][20].

8. **Optional focus beat (only with the end frame pinned, or the layout softens).** "Shallow depth of field on the slice; focus stays on the strands."
   Rationale: BFL and Runware lens language [12][17]. Avoid rack focus here: per point 4 it would defocus the layout (inference).

9. **Audio (if Palmier exposes it).** "Audio: a soft crust crackle as the slice tears free, quiet kitchen room tone, no music."
   Rationale: describe sound-implying action and layers [10][17].

10. **Close.** "No on-screen text added, no subtitles."
    Rationale: BFL's own example prompts end this way. Short, targeted negatives appear in BFL's video examples even though the image docs recommend positive phrasing [7][8][14].

**Workflow upgrade (not a clause).** For the slice lift, generate the last frame as a FLUX image edit of the designed still ("lift the hero slice with a hand, a cheese pull stretching; keep the doily, arch, tiles, motifs and paper exactly unchanged"), then run two-keyframe i2v at a pinned integer duration [8][15].
