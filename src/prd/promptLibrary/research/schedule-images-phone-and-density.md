# Phones, screens and frame density: research dossier
_Researched 2026-09-04. Sources numbered at the bottom; cite by number inline._

Scope: Figma image generation with GPT Image 2 (OpenAI) and Gemini 3 Pro Image / Nano Banana Pro (Google) behind it. Two reported faults in our "faceless person holding a phone, candid Indian lifestyle" outputs: the phone renders wrong, and compositions garble. Tiers: VENDOR = OpenAI, Google or Figma documentation; PRACTITIONER = a named person with a tested workflow or measurement; COMMUNITY = forum or curated-list claims. Anything I could not fetch is not a source. Where a page was reached only through a text-extraction proxy (r.jina.ai) that is noted in the source list; the content is the page's own.

Headline: no vendor documents phones, screens or hands-on-devices at all. The phone problem has to be solved from the general object-interaction and iteration guidance plus practitioner plate-shooting practice. The composition problem is much better covered: both vendors say iterate rather than overload, and OpenAI states in its own docs that the model "may have difficulty placing elements precisely in structured or layout-sensitive compositions" [1].

## Q1 Phones and screens (failure modes, then fixes, tiered)

### What the vendors document about devices and screens
- VENDOR, OpenAI. Nothing about phones, screens, bezels or hand-device contact in the API image guide [1], the GPT Image models prompting guide [2] or the gpt-image-1.5 guide [3]. The nearest items are general: "describe scale, body framing, gaze, and object interactions" with the example "hands naturally gripping the handlebars" [2]; for interfaces, "describe the product as if it already exists. Focus on layout, hierarchy, spacing, and real interface elements, and avoid concept art language" [2]; the 1.5 guide demonstrates placing a UI mockup "in an iPhone frame" [3]. The API guide also states the model "can still struggle with precise text placement and clarity" [1], which is directly relevant to anything rendered on a screen.
- VENDOR, Google. Nothing on phones or screens in the Gemini image docs [4], the 2.5 Flash Image prompting post [5], the Cloud "ultimate" guide [6] or the Nano Banana Pro tips [7]. The nearest items: "Define their physical makeup. Don't just ask for a suit jacket; ask for 'navy blue tweed'" (materiality) [6]; text on surfaces: "Be clear about the text, the font style (descriptively), and the overall design" [4]; and reference-image roles: "When using uploaded images, clearly define the role of each" [7].
- VENDOR, Figma. The Make-an-image help page says only "Try to include as much context as possible" and offers a Make changes loop plus reference images [9]. The AI-workflows collection lists reference-image uses including "A product or object you want included in the scene" and "A sketch or wireframe to guide composition and layout" [10]. The Gemini 3 Pro blog says "With Figma Weave, you can composite a number of images together" [11]. None of these mention phone mockups. Nothing solid found from any vendor on screen OFF versus flat colour versus green screen.

### Failure modes (practitioner and community evidence)
- Hands gripping objects is a known weak spot, not phone-specific: "A hand gripping a coffee mug might show fingers on both sides that do not add up correctly, or fingers that appear to pass through the mug rather than wrapping around it"; the model "struggles to infer 3D structure" once "fingers overlap, fold over each other, or grip an object" [14]. PRACTITIONER (detection guide, not a generation test).
- Distorted small objects lose structure ("That's not a violin. That's a plank of wood with spaghetti") and the fix offered is to name the object's parts ("A detailed violin with four strings, wooden body") [15]. PRACTITIONER, generic.
- Screen content: OpenAI's own docs concede text placement and clarity struggles [1]; a GPT Image 2 practitioner guide reports "Small labels, icon alignment, and exact data often need cleanup" and advises "For dense charts, reduce the number of labels and regenerate" [17]. INFERENCE: any live UI on a candid-shot phone is a text-and-layout problem at thumbnail scale, which is the worst case the vendors describe.
- Literal misreadings that add devices: on DALL-E 3, "close to the camera" "resulted in DALL-E adding a camera or a hand to the image" (Daller) [19]. COMMUNITY, older model, but a documented case of a device word summoning the device.
- Specific failure vocabulary the brief asked about (wrong proportions, extra buttons, mangled bezels, glowing rectangles): nothing solid found in fetched sources. Reviews claiming which model "rendered the most realistic phone" surfaced in search snippets but the pages were removed or truncated, so they are UNVERIFIED and not cited.

### Fixes that have evidence
1. Screen OFF / black glass is what real compositors shoot. Commercial photographer Tony Roslund's screen-replacement method (Fstoppers) shoots the scene and a separate dark plate; a commenter's summary of the practice: "Usually have client put the screen up as they want, then hit it with black foamcore for its own plate"; the composite uses Screen blend mode which "will preserve the specular highlights picked up in the original file" [13]. PRACTITIONER. INFERENCE for AI: a dark, reflective, switched-off glass is the plate that keeps the phone's speculars and needs no UI, so it is the safest ask.
2. Flat colour field or green screen: the evidence says do not rely on it. A Google staff generative-AI specialist measured Gemini 2.5 Flash Image against 50 hex codes: text-only hex gave average Delta E 11.25, an image swatch gave 0.90 [12]. A sprite practitioner prompting "EXACT hex #00FF00 (RGB 0, 255, 0) with NO gradients, NO noise, NO texture" still got "minor noise or gradient in the green background" and had to threshold it afterwards [16]. Green also spills: "the colored light that reflects back onto your subject from the green screen" [18]. PRACTITIONER. INFERENCE: a green rectangle on a candid phone invites spill onto fingers and uneven fill, and the model paints a screen anyway; black glass avoids both.
3. Name the phone as a specific known slab rather than "a phone". Practitioner phrasing that worked for mobile mockups on Nano Banana: "Modern iPhone 15/16 Pro Max with Dynamic Island, edge-to-edge display, no home button" [20]. A GPT Image 2 guide's mockup framework starts by declaring the device frame before content [17]. Tech Advisor got a believable device as the SUBJECT in one prompt with "no typical AI slop look" [21], which supports subject-first for the phone, not phone-as-detail. PRACTITIONER, mockup context, not hand-held candids; transfer is INFERENCE.
4. Write the hand-device contact as a physical relation, in the vendor's own idiom: OpenAI's "hands naturally gripping the handlebars" [2] and "looking down at the open book, not at the camera" [2] are the template; a hands guide independently advises explicit placements ("right hand resting on a table", "hands gripping a sword hilt") and, failing that, cropping "below elbows" [22]. PRACTITIONER + VENDOR. Nothing solid found that tests "thumb on the screen edge" specifically; it is INFERENCE from the same rule.
5. Hide or reduce the hands when they are not the point: "Hands partially in pockets" / "hands resting naturally by the side" [15]; "Adding human elements sparingly like hands ... give life without distracting" [23]. PRACTITIONER.
6. Generate without the live UI, composite afterwards. Figma documents compositing in Weave and reference-image roles [10][11], and OpenAI recommends describing UI "as if it already exists" only when the UI is the deliverable [2]. No vendor documents a phone-plate-then-mockup workflow. The practitioner analogue is Roslund's plate method [13]. A Figma Community "Realistic Hand Holding Phone" mockup file appeared in search results but was not fetched: UNVERIFIED.

### Hex colour codes as a steering lever
- OpenAI: no mention of hex codes anywhere in [1][2][3]. Colour is given as words and as "specify typography details (font style, size, color, placement)" [2].
- Google: no hex codes in [4][5][6][7]; colour is given descriptively ("muted teal tones", "1980s color film") [6].
- The only measured evidence is Bakkali's: text hex is roughly 12 times worse than a swatch on Gemini 2.5 Flash Image [12]; the sprite report agrees that an emphatic hex still drifts [16]. Community prompt lists do use hex in prompts (e.g. a "#562226" studio background) without evidence of accuracy [24]. Verdict: hex is not a documented lever for either vendor; a colour name plus a reference swatch is. GPT Image 2 specifically: nothing solid found.

## Q2 Element budget and composition

### What the vendors say
- OpenAI: "Iterate instead of overloading: Start with a clean base prompt, then refine with small, single-change follow-ups" [3]. "Write prompts in a consistent order (background/scene, subject, key details, constraints)" and for complex requests use "short labeled segments or line breaks instead of one long paragraph" [2][3]. "If layout matters, call out placement (e.g., 'logo top-right,' 'subject centered with negative space on left')" [2]. Limitation stated in the API guide: "the model may have difficulty placing elements precisely in structured or layout-sensitive compositions" [1]. Camera specs "may be interpreted loosely, so use them mainly for high-level look and composition" [2]. Avoid "words that imply studio polish or staging" for natural photos [2][3].
- Google: "Describe the scene, don't just list keywords" and "Be hyper-specific" [5]; Nano Banana Pro's five slots are one Subject, one Composition, one Action, one Location, one Style [7]; the photorealistic template is one "[shot type] of a [subject description] in a [setting description]" plus light and camera [4]; a minimalist composition is "a single [subject] positioned in the [location]" with "significant negative space" [4]. Positive framing: "'empty street' instead of 'no cars'" [6]. Imagen's input token limit is 480 tokens [8b] and in-image text should be 25 characters or fewer [8].
- Figma: think like a creative director and answer six questions (subject, shot type, composition, lighting, style, mood) [10]; edit "from broad to specific. Get the overall composition right first" [10].
- No vendor gives a numeric object budget. Nothing solid found.

### Practitioner and community numbers
- COMMUNITY (Daller, DALL-E 3 era): "roughly 30 to 40 graphical tokens can be maximally and correctly translated into a photo style. Beyond that, objects and colors start to degrade" and "the first described element is given slightly more attention", so "Write the most important thing first, then the details, and finally technical instructions" [19]. Unmeasured, older model.
- COMMUNITY (PaulBellow): "if you ask for a scene and don't mention number of people, it will try to fill it with hundreds of people" [25]. Older model; the lesson (state the count) matches vendor advice.
- PRACTITIONER: "focus on 3-5 primary components" and "keep the number of people in your image low and clearly describe poses" [26]. Unmeasured blog guidance.
- PRACTITIONER (mockups): per screen "six to eight distinct elements" before layouts crowd [20]; "reduce the number of labels and regenerate" [17].
- Verdict: no measured threshold exists for photoreal candids. The consistent practitioner band is 3 to 5 named things in frame, and every vendor structure allows exactly one subject, one action, one place.

### Camera angle and faceless framing
- Vendors list the vocabulary without warnings: "low angle shot" [7], "aerial view" [6], "from below", "aerial" [8], "slightly elevated 45-degree shot", "low perspective" [9b]. Nothing solid found from either vendor on POV-down-the-body, overhead or cropped framing degrading anatomy.
- PRACTITIONER (video, adjacent): first-person POV "Text prompts alone rarely produce a correct POV"; true overhead "Manual prompting tends to drift on extreme verticals"; the fix is a visual anchor, and "Get the composition approved as a static frame" first [27]. INFERENCE: the same two angles are the risky ones for stills.
- PRACTITIONER: "The full-body composition tends to be broken by the small size of the face" [28]; compositors say "not to crop them at the joints" [13]; hands guides recommend deliberate crops ("crop below elbows") rather than accidental ones [22].
- Faceless without a face problem: OpenAI's gaze instruction "looking down at the open book, not at the camera" [2] is the documented way to point a head away from camera; combine with a body-framing clause ("full body visible, feet included" style) [2].

## Q3 Iteration method
- VENDOR, OpenAI: "start with a clean base prompt and refine with small, single-change follow-ups ('make lighting warmer,' 'remove the extra tree,' 'restore the original background')" and "re-specify critical details if they start to drift" [2]. For edits: "'change only X' + 'keep everything else the same,' and repeat the preserve list on each iteration" [2]. In the Responses API the mainline model "will automatically revise your prompt for improved performance" and the rewritten text is exposed as the `revised_prompt` field [1]. State exclusions and invariants explicitly ("no watermark", "no extra text") [2]. Note: Figma's UI does not surface revised_prompt; whether Figma calls the Images API or the Responses tool is UNVERIFIED.
- VENDOR, Google: "Don't expect a perfect image on the first try. Use the conversational nature of the model to make small changes" [5]; "Multi-turn conversation is the recommended way to iterate on images" [9b]; if features drift after several turns, restart with a full detailed description [5]; edits should be "direct and specific" [7].
- VENDOR, Figma: Make changes on the result [9]; "Get the overall composition right first", then "targeted tools for precise structural edits" [10]; Nano Banana Pro can "refine not just regenerate" [11].
- COMMUNITY: to stop the model padding a deliberately sparse prompt, PaulBellow appends "DO NOT add any detail, just use it AS-IS" or "My prompt has full detail so no need to add more" [25]. Older model; the intent survives as "keep to the described elements".
- PRACTITIONER: "start with the core scene, then refine one variable at a time" [17].
- Sourced fix loop for our library: (1) strip to base = one subject, one action, one place, one light, phone described as black glass; (2) generate; (3) change exactly one clause per run and keep a preserve list; (4) when the phone is wrong, move the phone earlier in the prompt (first element gets more weight [19]) and name its make; (5) when layout is wrong, cut named objects until 3 to 5 remain; (6) when both fail, reset to base rather than stacking fixes [2][5].

## Proposed clause phrasings (concrete phone clauses, a frame-density rule, each with rationale + source #)
1. Phone clause (default): "an iPhone 16 Pro Max, matte black, edge-to-edge display, no home button, screen switched off, dark glass reflecting the room" placed as the second element after the subject. Rationale: named slab with concrete features [20][6]; screen off is the compositor's plate and drops the on-screen text task [13][1]; early position gets more weight [19].
2. Hand contact clause: "right hand wrapped around the back of the phone, thumb resting on the screen edge, looking down at the phone, not at the camera". Rationale: vendor object-interaction and gaze idiom [2]; hands gripping objects fail when the relation is unstated [14]. The thumb phrase is INFERENCE from the same rule, not tested in any fetched source.
3. Hand-minimised variant: "phone held loosely at waist height, hand mostly out of frame below the elbow crop". Rationale: hide or crop hands when they are not the point [15][22][23]; never crop at joints [13].
4. Screen-later variant for Figma: same as clause 1, then replace the dark screen with a real mockup in Figma. Rationale: Figma documents reference images and Weave compositing [10][11]; UI-on-screen is where vendors admit text and layout weakness [1][17]. No vendor workflow doc for this exact step: INFERENCE.
5. Colour clause: name the colour and, when it must match, attach a swatch reference; never rely on a hex string alone. Rationale: text hex Delta E 11.25 vs swatch 0.90 on Gemini [12]; emphatic hex still drifts [16]; neither vendor documents hex [1][2][4][6].
6. Frame-density rule: one subject, one action, one place cue, one light, plus the phone; no more than five named things in frame; constraints last. Rationale: vendors structure prompts as exactly one of each [7][4][10]; "Iterate instead of overloading" [3]; practitioner band of 3 to 5 components [26]; token-budget observations [19].
7. Angle rule: prefer eye-level, three-quarter or slightly elevated; treat POV-down-the-body and true overhead as high-risk and give them a reference image if used. Rationale: vendor angle vocabulary lists no warnings [6][7][8], the only failure evidence is for POV and overhead in a sibling medium [27], full-body shrinks faces [28].
8. Realism register: "real photograph, candid, in the moment" and no studio or staging words. Rationale: [3][2].
9. Fix-loop clause (for the library's method section): change one thing per run, restate the preserve list, reset to base after two failed stacks. Rationale: [2][5][17].
