# Zepto campaign tiles: phone-in-hand tile, research dossier
_Researched 2026-08-16. Sources at the bottom, numbered; cite by number inline._

Scope: ONE thing, how pros shoot a smartphone held in a hand with the screen left blank so a designer drops the UI in later, plus a quick skim of how the phone is normally shown in grocery / quick-commerce campaign photography.

## What the medium actually is
- The technique is called **screen replacement** (video and stills), and the shooting-side rule is: shoot the phone with the display **off**, so it photographs as a **black, glossy plate**. The PremiumBeat tutorial: "I purposely shot this on a blank screen so I'll be able to use the black color to help easily isolate the reflection later" [1]. Adobe After Effects expert Roei Tzoref: "The black screen would act like a mirror and give you reflections you could use and natural highlights", and "I also would not ever shoot a shot like this with a green screen on the phone" [2].
- Why not green: "often green screens on phones can end up creating green spill and overexposing the image", so a darker fill is preferable; ActionVFX sells its screen-marker packs in five colours because "background color choice depends on lighting conditions and color contrast" [3]. A neutral grey with a little noise is the named middle ground: "you can have the reflection but also get the exact framing" and it still throws fill light onto the hand [2].
- Tracking markers exist for moving shots ("tracking markers would be required if there is no stark contrast between objects and the background, or if you have nothing but a blurry or glossy surface to work with" [3]) but the same expert says of drawn crosses on a phone: "don't create these crosses...you will have to cover over", the phone's own edges track fine [2]. For a still tile, markers are irrelevant.
- The reflection is not killed, it is **kept and re-layered**: duplicate the plate, matte the screen, and composite the reflection over the inserted UI with Lighten / Add / Screen blend modes, then tune opacity [1]. Photoshop tutorials that lack a real reflection fake one with a white shape at 10 to 20% opacity [17], which is exactly the "generic" look to avoid.
- Glare on the glass is controlled the way any glossy product is: big overhead softbox, move the product or camera until the reflection of the rig disappears, black cards / flags to absorb stray light, microfibre for fingerprints and dust, cloning in post for leftovers [6]. Polariser on the phone glass specifically: nothing solid found (only generic polariser articles surfaced); mark as inference.
- Apple's own marketing rules are the counter-weight: for App Store creative "Don't display a blank screen on an Apple product", "Straight-on product shots are preferred. Don't use extreme angles", no added "reflections, shadows, highlights" on the supplied renders, no 3D rendering or simulation of the device, and generic-device illustrations must not carry Apple-unique details (Home button, sensor housing, Ring/Silent switch, volume controls) [5]. So a blank-screen plate is a WORKING file, never the shipped tile, and the shipped tile should be a straight-on-ish, non-Apple-branded phone.
- Grocery / quick-commerce campaign conventions (held up, angle, thumb): **nothing solid found**. Searches returned ad-platform strategy pieces, not craft. One behavioural finding transfers: "Product attitudes and sales increase when you show a hand touching your product from the viewer's point of view" (paywalled summary, no study details visible) [18]. UNVERIFIED beyond that sentence.

## What separates a convincing result from a generic one
- Reflection is real, not painted: a soft window or softbox highlight sliding across the black glass, not a diagonal white bar at 15% [1, 2, 17].
- Screen is a clean, even dark plate with **no** invented icons or text; the retoucher wants nothing to remove [1, 2].
- Grip: "firm yet delicate", fingers relaxed and slightly curved, never clenched ("Watch out for clenched hands"), thumb resting on the bezel or side rail rather than across the display, product held square to the lens [7, 10, 11].
- Grooming: "Clean nails are a must", clear polish at most, no gel or acrylic, moisturised skin, jewellery and wrist tattoos removed, neutral clothing with no logos, hand tone matched to the rest of the body [7, 8, 9, 10].
- Angle: straight-on or slightly elevated, no extreme tilt [5]; the 45-degree elevated product angle is the model docs' own example phrasing [13].
- Camera language the docs reward: name the lens and aperture ("85mm portrait lens", "shallow depth of field (f/1.8)"), the light ("three-point softbox setup"), and the camera body [12, 13]. Lens for phone-in-hand specifically (85 to 100mm) is UNVERIFIED as a documented convention; treat as inference from portrait-lens practice.
- Non-Apple device: generic slab phone without Apple-unique hardware cues, so the tile is safe to ship [5].

## Model-side levers
- OpenAI docs (gpt-image-2 / 1.5 / 1): the model "can still struggle with precise text placement and clarity" and "may have difficulty placing elements precisely in structured or layout-sensitive compositions"; no hand or blank-screen guidance is published; gpt-image-2 "doesn't currently support transparent backgrounds" [15]. Third-party GPT Image 2 guide: give an explicit constraint slot ("no watermark / no logos / no extra text"), because unbounded briefs "fail silently"; UI only renders well when "screen type, copy, hierarchy, and spacing are all explicit", which cuts the other way too: say nothing about UI and the model will still fill the screen unless told what the screen IS [16].
- Google (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image / Nano Banana): "Use positive framing: Describe what you want, not what you don't want (e.g. 'empty street' instead of 'no cars')" [12, 13]; think like a photographer, "camera angles, lens types, lighting, and fine details" [13]; text may be misspelt, stylisation "can sometimes be inconsistent" [14]. Hands: not addressed in either Google guide beyond consistency caveats [12, 13, 14]. So describe the empty screen as a positive material ("a switched-off phone, screen a uniform dark glossy black reflecting a soft window highlight"), never as "no UI".
- The "explicit ask for a flat colour" is therefore an inference from [12, 13, 16], not a documented vendor recommendation. UNVERIFIED that any vendor doc says "keep the screen an empty flat colour".

## Lifestyle scenarios for the phone-in-hand tile
_Appended 2026-08-16. Seven scenarios, each needing a physically stated light clause + setting vocabulary. Same rule as above: the screen stays a flat plate, UI comes later._

### A. Night, phone as the key light
- Vocabulary is film-side. "Practical" = a light source visible in frame ("lamps, candles, or even the television set"); "motivated lighting" = light that imitates a plausible source, and the standing rule is "Make sure color temperatures match" [20]. Screen glow specifically: real screens are "not bright enough or have a lot of flicker" [23], "the actual light from a TV isn't sufficient or controllable" [19], so DPs hide "small LED lights smaller than smartphones" behind the device and keep the light **motivated**, i.e. coming from where the phone is [19]. The look is built as blue-ish light against a tungsten-balanced room: "tungsten light corrected to daylight with camera balanced at tungsten setting" gives "the familiar blue light" of a screen [19]. Deakins' note that an LED panel "creates a similar soft light from a small unit" is the softness cue: soft but SMALL source [19].
- Why the split is warm/cool: sRGB and every consumer display target D65, "6500 Kelvin (a.k.a. D65) is commonly used as a standard illuminant / white point" and is "fairly representative of daylight"; simulating incandescent on a monitor "would result in an extremely yellow-orange display" [21]. So a phone face is daylight-white; a bedside bulb is orange; in one frame that IS mixed colour temperature. Kelvin for tungsten bulbs (about 2700 to 3200K) is not stated on a fetched page, treat as common knowledge, INFERENCE.
- Falloff: "The intensity of light is inversely proportional to the square of the distance", a source held close gives "a steep light fall-off" with the background staying dark [22]. A phone 40 cm from a face is the textbook close small source: bright chin/fingers, black room two feet away.
- Keeping the flat-colour screen from blowing out: nothing solid found as a written rule. The compositing convention above (screen off, black plate) plus the DP habit of putting the glow on a hidden dimmed source [19] gives the phrasing: the glow is described on the HAND and FACE, the screen itself is described as an even, dim, matte-lit plate. INFERENCE.

### B. Commute, evening
- From a moving vehicle the sharp-subject rule is shutter, not tripod: "at least 1/400th of a second or faster to stop motion", ISO 800 up, "Hand hold the camera and don't lean against the car", and near foreground/roadside detail blurs anyway even at those speeds [24]. So hand + phone crisp, window world streaked is physically honest.
- Blue hour: "the period of transition from civil to nautical twilight", peak "just a few minutes", the payoff is "the contrast between the cool blue hues of the ambient light and the warmth of artificial lighting", and "There are just a few minutes when the artificial and ambient lighting is perfectly balanced" [25]. Sodium-vapour vs LED street-light colour: nothing solid found on a fetched page; "warm artificial vs cool sky" is the citable form.

### C. Group around one phone
- Lifestyle craft: give people an action ("asking subjects to perform an action or have a conversation"), layer them ("some people sit while others stand"), use "window light indoors", vary the angle ("Get low ... or shoot from above") [27]; "It's not always about a person's expression. What they are actually doing can be just as important", "Don't worry about chopping off heads", "Life is messy and sometimes that makes the difference in believing the story" [26]. Stock convention for the phone itself is the over-the-shoulder plate: "The device is held in a hand, and the background shows a blurred interior setting", screen "designed for easy replacement" [28]. Brand-specific staging of several people on ONE phone: nothing solid found beyond these; "hands entering frame" is inference from [26, 28].

### D. Kitchen morning window light
- The food/lifestyle setup: window as the only source, "Side light is where light comes from the side of the subject and your camera", diffuser between window and subject, "A reflector bounces light from the light source onto the subject. This is what is known as fill light", soft shadows have "soft edges" [29]. North light: "a large North-facing window and the light coming in is always gorgeous, even on cloudy days", white foam board fill on the shadow side, "make your light as big as possible" [30]. Backlight (window behind the subject) is the other named food setup [30].

### E. Model-side guidance for POV hands and groups
- Google Nano Banana Pro: name framing "like a cinematographer", the doc's own list includes "POV shot" and "A low-angle shot with a shallow depth of field (f/1.8)"; it can "Maintain the consistency and resemblance of multiple characters, even when they appear together in a group", but "character consistency across edits may vary" and "lighting changes can sometimes produce unnatural artifacts"; hands not addressed [31]. Vertex Imagen guide: modifiers "close up, taken from far away", "aerial, from below", lighting "natural, dramatic, warm, cold", settings "motion blur, soft focus, bokeh, portrait", and for people "Specify facial details as a focus of the photo (for example, use the word 'portrait')" [33]. Third-party: "Keep natural skin texture, believable hands", and in a group "identify the target by position and visible attributes instead of ... 'the woman'"; fix by "one focused correction" not a fresh prompt [32]. GPT Image 2 guide (third-party): a hand-only POV is done as "subtle player hand in the lower right", and mixed light is written literally, "cool overhead train light mixed with warm town lights", "incandescent work lamp spilling warm light" [16]. Hand-count or finger-count guidance from OpenAI or Google: nothing solid found; state hand count and whose hand it is, INFERENCE from [16, 32].

### Where the scenarios fight the group constant
The constant "one hard sun, shadows tinted violet" holds cleanly only for 4 and, weakened, 6. Scenarios 1, 3, 5, 7 have no sun at all and 2 has a sky but no sun. Rule of thumb: keep the "one key source" half of the constant (one practical, one window, one screen), drop "hard sun", and move the violet from "shadow tint" to the cool half of the warm-cool split, stated positively per [12, 13] ("cool violet-blue ambient on the far side of the hand"), never as "no sun". INFERENCE, not a documented convention.

## Realism: why generated lifestyle photos read as AI, and the levers
_Appended 2026-08-16. Question: the bedroom / metro / friends-round-a-table tiles from GPT Image 2 and Nano Banana "look AI". What the tells are, what the vendors and credible practitioners say moves the needle, and whether "phone snapshot" framing beats "editorial"._

### The tells (documented)
- **Academic taxonomy** (Kamali, Nakamura, Chatzimparmpas, Hullman, Groh; Northwestern / MIT Media Lab lineage): five categories, "anatomical, stylistic, functional, violations of physics, and sociocultural" [36]. The stylistic bucket is the one that bites lifestyle tiles: "Does this have that sheen? Does this just look oversaturated in some way?", waxy appearance, overly perfect skin, cinematic lighting mismatches, smudgy patches [34]; "waxy or glossy skin, missing teeth", and images that are "overly perfect and cinematographic, featuring people with symmetrical, classically beautiful features" that look "too good to be true" [35]. Groh's inversion is the useful frame: "a real image is something that has the absence of artifacts" [35]. Hands: models "are less adept at hands", "odd proportions" [34], though 2024-era.
- **Journalism / media-literacy**: Poynter MediaWise names "weird hands", "odd reflections or shadows", "oddities in backgrounds" [37]. Physics tells: "shadows falling at conflicting angles", mirrors that disagree with the scene [34].
- **Practitioner lists** (third-party, corroborate the above with more granularity): plastic pore-less skin, dead eyes without catchlights, "symmetrical everything", impossible lighting, gibberish text, "background soup", "too clean, too perfect", uncanny teeth, wax-figure hair, "the AI sheen" (high saturation + contrast) [39]; "the model's default is airbrushed", "generic stock composition" ("white-collar smiling at the camera", centred, neutral backdrop), lighting "too clean" with no shadow falloff or environmental texture [40]. HDR-ish global contrast, uniform sharpness, textureless fabric: named only in these practitioner lists, not in the academic or journalism sources fetched; treat as corroborated-by-practice, not forensic.
- OpenAI's own guide implicitly agrees: it warns off language that "imply studio polish or staging" [38]. Nothing solid found from Google naming tells; Google only says stylisation "can sometimes be inconsistent" [14].

### The levers (vendor-documented first, then practitioner)
- OpenAI cookbook (gpt-image-2 primary): "prompt the model as if a real photo is being captured in the moment", use "photography language (lens, lighting, framing)", ask "for real texture (pores, wrinkles, fabric wear, imperfections)", avoid words that "imply studio polish or staging"; its exemplar is a "photorealistic candid photograph" with "weathered skin with visible wrinkles, pores, and sun texture", "Shot like a 35mm film photograph ... 50mm lens", "subtle film grain, natural color balance", and "should feel honest and unposed, with real skin texture, worn materials" [38]. It does NOT mention 8k / hyperreal or negative prompting [38].
- Google Gemini 2.5 Flash Image: the photorealistic template ends "Captured with a [camera/lens details], emphasizing [key textures and details]"; be "hyper-specific", "Describe the scene, don't just list keywords", positive framing; its exemplar leads with "deep, sun-etched wrinkles" [13]. Vertex Imagen guide: lens table (portraits "24-35mm", prime/zoom), settings "motion blur, soft focus, bokeh", film types "black and white photo, polaroid", lighting "natural, dramatic, warm, cold"; it also recommends "high-quality, beautiful, stylized" modifiers, which for THIS goal is the wrong direction (Imagen was written for polish, not candour); its negative-prompt rule is "state the thing plainly (wall, frame), no directive language" [33].
- Practitioner consensus (third-party): name a real body + lens + ISO ("Canon EOS R5, 85mm f/1.8, ISO 200") because settings "reintroduce the imperfections our eyes read as real" [43]; "35mm film, visible skin pores in close detail, subtle film grain" against waxy skin, present-tense verbs ("mid-laugh") against stock composition, and "one environmental imperfection" (dust, fingerprint, asymmetric shadow) stacked on a named material and a named light against rendered-looking lighting [40]; "slightly wrinkled shirt", "loose strands of hair", "coffee ring on the desk", "natural facial asymmetry", "natural teeth, slightly off-white", one consistent named light source, name film stocks ("Portra 400") [39]. An explicit banned-word list ("beautiful/perfect/8k/hyperreal") from a vendor: nothing solid found; the closest is OpenAI's "studio polish or staging" warning [38] and Imagen's own use of "beautiful" [33]. Marked INFERENCE that dropping them helps; consistent with [38, 39, 40].

### "Phone snapshot" vs "editorial"
- The evidence is community, not vendor. The viral 2025 GPT-4o prompt (r/singularity, reposted widely): "an extremely unremarkable iPhone selfie with no clear subject or framing just a careless snapshot", "a touch of motion blur", "mildly overexposed from uneven sunlight", "angle is awkward, the composition nonexistent", "aggressively mediocre, like a photo taken by accident" [41]. Cybernews' write-up (403 on fetch, seen only in search snippets, NOT cited) reports the outputs read as real because of "natural lighting and imperfections". Practitioner extension: "cheap smartphone camera", "slightly blurry", "grain from low light", "off-center", "awkward angle", "harsh fluorescent lighting", "candid", plus lens flare, compression artifacts, washed-out colour [42].
- Trade-off for a campaign tile: the same source concedes ChatGPT / Gemini alone drift back to "more stylized, polished images" [42], and the amateur recipe buys realism by spending composition, exposure and colour, which a tile with a composited UI, brand colour and a legibility budget cannot afford. Nothing solid found measuring the two head-to-head. Working rule (INFERENCE): keep the editorial FRAME (deliberate crop, one motivated light, phone screen a flat plate) but import the snapshot's SURFACE (skin, fabric, grain, clutter, slight asymmetry, motion in the non-subject plane). Kellogg's finding cuts the same way: the give-away is "too good to be true" [35], not the framing per se.
