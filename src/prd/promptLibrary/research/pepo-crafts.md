# Pepo crafts · research dossier
_Researched 2026-10-02. Sources listed at the bottom, numbered; cite by number inline._

Scope: the six real crafts behind the Pepo House visual system (Jaipur blue pottery, Rajasthani
miniature painting, jharokha architecture, lace doilies, Rajasthani weaving and block printing,
print texture). Honesty notes: the Met (Vercel bot checkpoint, HTTP 429), Britannica, the British

## What the medium actually is

**1. Jaipur blue pottery.** Not clay. A body of quartz stone powder, powdered glass, Multani
mitti (Fuller's earth), borax, gum and water; Gaatha calls it the only pottery in the world that
does not use clay [1][2][3]. The dough is rolled into a 4 to 5 mm "chapatti", pressed into moulds,
smoothed, dipped in a quartz-powder slip, painted with cobalt oxide mixed with gum, glazed with a
fritted mix of powdered glass, borax, zinc oxide, potassium nitrate and boric acid, then low-fired
in a closed charcoal kiln at 800 to 850 C [1]. Wikipedia compares the frit body to Egyptian
faience [2]. Palette: cobalt blue, copper-oxide green, white, occasionally turquoise, yellow,
brown or black [1][2][3]. Motifs: floral, geometric, birds, animals, Persian geometric and Mughal
arabesque [1][2][3]. Products: tiles, vases, plates, door knobs, surahi-type pots, coasters [1][2].
AIACA names the authenticity tell: a translucent blue glaze with "uneven brush strokes" from hand
painting [3]. GI status: listed as GI application 66, registered in the April 2008 to March 2009
period [35]; AIACA says "GI tag in 2023" [3]. The two disagree; treat the date as UNRESOLVED.

**2. Rajasthani (Rajput) miniature painting.** Opaque watercolour (gouache) and gold on paper
[10][11][12]. Process per [12]: charcoal sketch, refined in sanguine, white priming, black
outline, colour laid in layers with burnishing between coats for lustre, then *khulai* ("opening
up", fine detailing), shading, gold, and *moti mahavar*, raised impasto dots on jewellery and
ornaments. Squirrel-tail-hair brushes for faces; white primer coat; burnishing with polishing
stones; borders left blank and painted last [13]. Pigments confirmed by Raman analysis in Mughal
and Rajput miniatures: massicot, red lead, lead white, vermilion, litharge, Indian yellow [14].
Indian yellow is a transparent, luminous orange-yellow (euxanthate salts), its cow-urine origin
supported by a 2018 study [15]. Lapis lazuli / ultramarine in Rajput work: nothing solid found
from a fetched primary page (search snippets only; UNVERIFIED). Early Rajput style: profile view,
figures "heavily outlined in black", little modelling, colours "saturated and opaque"; the
perspective uses "several viewpoints"; one portrait type is the "window portrait", a seated
figure framed in a window [11]. Conservative schools (Mewar) keep "flatness, abstraction, bold
line and bright colour"; Kishangarh is among the Mughal-derived schools [12].
- *Kishangarh*: "aquiline profiles and exaggerated almond-shaped eyes", nocturnal lakeside scenes
  in jewel tones, red barge, gilded pavilions, blue-skinned Krishna figure [10].
- *Bundi*: lush vegetation, dramatic night skies, water drawn as light swirls on a dark ground,
  courtyards and pavilions, round faces and receding foreheads [16] (travel blog, secondary).
  The peacock dancing in rain as a Bundi hallmark: search snippet only, UNVERIFIED.
- *Jaipur (Amber)*: zenith under Sawai Pratap Singh (1779 to 1803) per search snippets only;
  nothing solid fetched. The same ruler commissioned the Hawa Mahal in 1799 [19].
- *Hashiya* (border/margin, Persian for edge or selvedge): definition seen only in a search
  snippet from imp-art.org, whose page returned 403. UNVERIFIED but low-risk.

**3. Jharokha architecture.** A stone window projecting from an upper-storey wall, carried on two
or more brackets or corbels, with two pillars, a balustrade and a cupola or pyramidal roof; later
ones add *jaali* lattice screens, deep *chhajja* sun-shades and *chhatri* domes, and use
"scalloped or cusped arches borrowed from Mughal designs" [17]. A multifoil (cusped, polylobed,
scalloped) arch has foils/lobes/cusps cut into its inner curve; trefoil, cinquefoil and so on;
"multifoil" strictly means more than five foils; common in Mughal and Rajput work under Shah Jahan
[18]. Hawa Mahal: 953 jharokhas in a honeycomb of semi-octagonal bays, red and pink sandstone,
domed canopies, fluted pillars [19]. "Engrailed arch" as a term: nothing solid found; it is a
heraldic word, and the architectural sources use cusped/multifoil/scalloped (inference: prefer
those).

**4. Lace doilies.** A doily is an ornamental openwork mat in cotton or linen thread, crocheted,
tatted, knitted, or paper; many are radially symmetric, worked from the centre outward; others are
filet on a square grid [27]. Lace anatomy: the mesh is the *ground* or *réseau*, solid parts the
*toilé*, a thicker outlining thread the *gimp* or *cordonnet* [24]. *Picot*: a small loop of
thread on the edge of lace, crochet or tatting [28]. *Guipure* laces have no mesh ground, motifs
joined by plaits or bars [24]. Needle lace is built from buttonhole/blanket stitch [29].
Battenberg is a tape lace: machine-woven tape tacked to a pattern, spaces filled with needle
stitches; the original used only buttonhole picot, later "flat wheel (spider or rosette)" and
rings [23][36]. Filet lace: darning (*point de reprise*) or linen stitch (*point de toile*) on a
knotted square-mesh net [25]. Filet crochet: chain-stitch mesh with some squares filled by double
crochet, read from a two-colour grid, "pixel-like", traditionally white or ecru mercerized cotton
[26].

**5. Woven and printed folk grids.**
- *Pattu* (Meghwal weavers, Karwa near Jaisalmer): wool woven on a pit loom (*khaddi*) in narrow
  strips; motifs in extra weft that "looks like kashida embroidery": *machli* (fish), *burdi*
  (hut), *tataiya* (wasp), *chatri*, *patti* (stripes), *chida* (bird), trees; natural brown and
  white wool, now also red, pink, orange; horizontal border bands along both edges [20].
- *Panja dhurrie* (Salawas, near Jodhpur): flat-woven, weft-faced, reversible, packed with the
  *panja* comb; squares, diamonds, triangles, zigzags, chevrons [21].
- Camel motif: printed on dhotis and turbans for Banjaras at Bagru, alongside *chaupar*, horse,
  dove [5]. No fetched source names a woven camel motif in durrie or pattu: nothing solid found.
- *Kantha* is Bengali and Odia, not Rajasthani: running-stitched layered old saris with a rippled
  texture [22]. Keep it out of a Jaipur prompt (inference).
- *Sanganeri* (GI 147, registered 2009 to 2010 [35]): white base, "delicate floral motifs",
  butis, butas, *jhar*, intertwisted tendrils; mainly red and black with yellow, brown, indigo,
  green; colours "may overlap or circumscribe" one another; blocks cut by *kharadiyes* [4].
- *Bagru* (GI 183, registered 2011 to 2012 [35]; Wikipedia says 2024 [9], treat as conflicting):
  base "off white or fawn" from *harda* (myrobalan) tannin, which reads yellowish [5][6]; black
  *syahi/syahee* (rusted iron and jaggery) printed with the outline block *rekh*, red *begar*
  (alum) printed with the filler block, both developed in alizarin to black and red [5]; *dabu*
  mud resist (clay, lime, gum, wheat) for indigo, sprinkled with sawdust, and "no sharp
  definitions are achieved" [5]; motifs *leher* (waves), *chaupad* (checks), *kangura*
  (triangles), *jaali* (trellis) [6].
- Block anatomy: *gudh* background, *rekh* outline, *datta* filler; printer *chhipa*, dyer
  *rangrez*, washer *dhobi* [7][8]. Handmade tells: dye drips and alignment variation between
  block repeats [6].

**6. Print texture.**
- *Risograph*: a stencil duplicator. A master is burned, wrapped on a drum, soy ink pushed
  through, one spot colour per pass; only uncoated stock, roughly 60 to 300 gsm; "toothy" vellum
  finish works well [30][31]. Registration "will never be perfect", worse on thick paper; large
  block colour "will not be even in tone"; roller marks near the leading edge; output modes include
  "Grain Touch" and halftone [31]. Named inks include Fluorescent Pink, Metallic Gold, Blue, Mint,
  Kelly Green, Bright Red, Yellow [30][31].
- *Letterpress*: relief printing; traditional "kiss" impression barely marks the sheet; modern
  work deliberately debosses into soft cotton paper [32][33]. "Type should only go halfway
  through the paper" [32].
- *Kraft*: paper from kraft-process chemical pulp, naturally brown unless bleached, high tear
  strength [34]. Recycled-kraft flecks: nothing solid found.

## What separates a convincing result from a generic one

- **Blue pottery**: generic output is Delft or Chinese blue-and-white porcelain: hard, glassy, thin
  linework, pure white. Real Jaipur pieces have a thick, slightly soft frit body, a milky-white
  ground, cobalt plus green and turquoise, Persian floral and arabesque, and visibly uneven brush
  strokes [1][3]. (Inference: name "not porcelain, not Delft" once; it is a real model default.)
- **Miniature painting**: generic output is "Indian art" with soft digital shading and a single
  vanishing point. Real Rajput work: flat saturated opaque fields, black outline, profile faces,
  several viewpoints at once, burnished sheen, gold, raised pearl dots, painted borders [11][12][13].
  School matters: Kishangarh faces and Bundi water are recognisable at a glance [10][16].
- **Jharokha**: generic output is a pointed "Moorish" arch or a wooden balcony. Real: a projecting
  stone box on carved brackets, cusped arch, jaali screen, chhajja eave, small dome or chhatri on
  top [17][18]. In Jaipur, pink sandstone and repetition in a honeycomb [19].
- **Lace**: generic output is "lace texture" with no structure. Real lace has a legible ground
  vs solid (réseau vs toilé), an outlining cordonnet, and a picot or scalloped rim [24][28].
  Doilies radiate from a centre [27]; filet is square-gridded [26].
- **Weaving**: generic "tribal pattern" mixes Navajo, kilim and Aztec. Rajasthani pattu is
  discrete extra-weft motifs on plain natural wool with stripe bands [20]; durrie is weft-faced
  geometry, diamonds and chevrons [21].
- **Block print**: generic output is perfectly registered vector repeats. Real: visible block-step
  repeats, slight misalignment of the rekh outline over the fill, dye drips, a white (Sanganer) or
  harda off-white/fawn (Bagru) ground, soft-edged dabu areas [4][5][6].
- **Print texture**: generic "grain overlay" is uniform noise. Real riso has uneven solids,
  misregistered spot colours and uncoated tooth [30][31]; real letterpress has an impression you
  can see as a shadowed bite, not just a flat ink [32][33].

## Model-side levers

From the cross-cutting dossier `models.md` [37], not re-researched here:
- Specificity about material, texture and medium beats generic boosters [37].
- Keep a consistent clause order and put non-negotiables early; long prompts drop late clauses [37].
- Prefer positive description; reserve negations for where the model actually defaults wrong [37].
  Here that is: porcelain/Delft for blue pottery, 3D shading for miniatures, Navajo/Aztec for
  weaving (inference).
- Global rule: "No text, letters, numbers or labels anywhere in the image" (`rules.js`). So the
  letterpress and riso clauses must describe impression and ink on ornament, rules and blocks of
  colour, never on type. Do not ask for a hashiya with calligraphy.
- Obscure native terms (khulai, harda, rekh) should travel with a short gloss of what they look
  like, per the skill's clause contract (inference that the bare term alone is weak).

## Proposed clause upgrades

1. **Blue pottery surface.** "Jaipur blue pottery: a hand-painted quartz-frit tile, not clay, not
   porcelain; cobalt-oxide blue and copper green arabesque florals on a milky white slip, under a
   translucent low-fired glaze with slightly uneven brushstrokes and soft pooling at the edges."
   Rationale: names the body, both oxides, the ground and the hand tell. [1][2][3]
2. **Blue pottery short.** "Jaipur blue pottery tile: cobalt and turquoise Persian florals on milky
   quartz-frit white, glossy hand-brushed glaze." [1][3]

   burnished paper: flat saturated colour fields, fine black outlines, figures in profile, no
   modelled shading, several viewpoints in one scene." [11][12][13]
4. **Miniature palette.** "Palette of vermilion, red lead, lead white, luminous Indian yellow,
   deep blue and shell gold; tiny raised white dots on jewellery." Rationale: confirmed pigments
   plus moti mahavar glossed as raised dots. Deep blue left unnamed because lapis is unverified.
   [12][14][15]

   exaggerated almond eye, jewel tones against a dark night lake." [10]

   dense plantain and flowering trees." Plantain trees: snippet only, UNVERIFIED; drop if a render
   misreads. [16]

   a small repeating floral vine." Rationale: border painted last, separate from the image [13];
   the term hashiya is UNVERIFIED so it is not relied on alone.
8. **Jharokha frame.** "A Rajput jharokha: a projecting pink-sandstone window on carved stone
   brackets, a cusped multifoil arch, a pierced jaali screen below, a deep chhajja eave and a
   small domed chhatri on top." [17][18][19]

10. **Doily.** "A white cotton crochet doily worked in rounds from the centre: open mesh ground,
    solid petal motifs outlined by a thicker thread, a scalloped edge finished with tiny picot
    loops." [24][27][28]
11. **Filet grid (pixel bridge).** "Filet crochet in ecru cotton: a square mesh where filled
    squares draw the motif and open squares form the ground, like a stitched pixel chart." [26]
12. **Battenberg variant.** "Battenberg tape lace: flat woven tape curved into scrolls, the gaps
    bridged by buttonhole bars and spider-wheel fillings." [23][36]
13. **Pattu band.** "Rajasthani pattu weave: natural brown and white wool with small raised
    extra-weft motifs like embroidery (fish, bird, hut, stripe), set in horizontal border bands."
    [20]
14. **Durrie field.** "Flat weft-faced cotton durrie, densely packed: diamonds, triangles,
    zigzags and chevrons in crisp stepped edges." [21]
15. **Sanganeri print.** "Sanganeri hand block print on white cotton: small floral butis and
    tendril jaal in red and black, with each repeat a block-width step and faint misalignment of
    outline over fill." [4][7]
16. **Bagru print.** "Bagru hand block print: harda-yellowed off-white cotton, black iron outline
    and madder-red filler, slightly offset between blocks, occasional dye drips." [5][6]
17. **Dabu.** "Dabu mud-resist indigo: pale motifs with soft, cracked, slightly blurred edges on a
    deep indigo ground." [5]
18. **Riso texture.** "Risograph-printed on uncoated toothy stock: two soy spot inks slightly
    misregistered, grainy uneven solids, faint roller marks." No type. [30][31]
19. **Letterpress texture.** "Letterpress ornament debossed into soft cotton paper, the edges of
    each rule and block catching a slight shadow; ink a little heavier at the edges." Ink
    squeeze at edges: inference, no source found. [32][33]
20. **Kraft ground.** "Unbleached brown kraft paper ground with visible fibre." Fibre visibility
    is inference. [34]
